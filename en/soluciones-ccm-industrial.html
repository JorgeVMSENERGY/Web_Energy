Total control of your engines, from a single point.

VMS Energy designs, supplies and installs industrial CCMs: fixed, plug-in and Pilift types; forms 1 to 4b; DOL, soft-Sterger and VFD starters; Modbus, Profibus, EtherNet/IP and PROFINET communication. The applicable regulations are defined according to the specific requirements of each project.

A stationary engine costs more than the MCC that protects it.

In industrial facilities with dozens or hundreds of dispersed motors, the individual management of starters, protections and control wiring generates high maintenance costs, long diagnostic times and arc flash risks for personnel. When a critical process engine fails without coordinated protection, the impact is not limited to the equipment: it drags down entire production lines.

A well-specified industrial MCC according to IEC 61439 centralizes control, standardizes protections, facilitates secure access for maintenance and enables integration with the plant control system — transforming reactive fault management into predictive maintenance with real-time visibility.

VMS Energy has 18 years of experience in EPC electric projects in Mexico: Oil & Gas, power generation, manufacturing and mining. We design the MCC from detail engineering to commissioning and testing the FAT/SAT, with complete coordination of protections and NFPA 70E electric arc analysis.

Forms of separation IEC 61439-2

The form of separation defines the level of internal insulation between bars, functional units and terminals. Determines the safety during maintenance and the cost of the board.

Form 1

No internal separation

Distribution bars, functional units and cable terminals in a common compartment. Any intervention requires cutting the general power supply to the MCC.

Use: auxiliary low criticality facilities, service pumps, general ventilation.
Form 2a / 2b

Separate UF bars

The main bars are isolated from the functional units by barriers. Form 2a: no separation between adjacent FUs. Form 2b: with separation between each UF.

Usage: food and beverage industry, HVAC, water treatment plants.
Form 3a / 3b

Cable terminals separated from bars

Adds separation between external terminals and bus bars. Form 3b also includes separation between adjacent functional units. The most specified in medium manufacturing.

Usage: automotive manufacturing, mining, industrial distribution substations.
Form 4a / 4b

Total separation — maximum security

Bars, functional units and terminals in completely independent compartments. Form 4b: terminals in compartment separate from the UF — allows hot withdrawal (drawout).

Use: Oil & Gas, power generation, petrochemical plants — maintenance without production stoppage.

Constructive types of MCC

Each construction type defines the extraction mechanism and the level of operational continuity that it can guarantee during maintenance.

Fixed type MCC

Functional units are wired directly to the cabinet. Removal of any module requires cutting power to the main bus or affected cubicle. It is the option with the lowest initial cost and greatest construction simplicity.

  • Available forms: 1, 2a/2b, 3a/3b.
  • Typical starters: DOL up to 75 kW, star-delta up to 200 kW.
  • Maintenance requires stopping the process or using by-pass disconnectors.
  • Suitable when the controlled motors are non-critical process or have redundancy.
  • Integrated brands: ABB, Schneider Electric, Eaton.

MCC plug-in type

The functional units are connected to the bus via plug-in contacts. They can be removed without special tools and without cutting the main bus — although the cubicle remains under tension during removal. Combines economy and maintenance flexibility.

  • Forms available: 2b, 3a/3b, 4a.
  • Typical starters: DOL, Y-Δ, soft-starter up to 630 kW, VFD up to 400 kW integrated.
  • Module removal in seconds without cutting the bus — ideal for frequent startups of spare modules.
  • Personnel must wear arc flash PPE (NFPA 70E Cal/cm² as per study) during extraction.
  • Integrated brands: Siemens SIRIUS, ABB MNS, Schneider Prisma.

Each functional unit is mounted on a removable cart with three operating positions: Connected (normal operation), Test (control circuit active, power off) and Offline (car outside the cubicle). Allows complete maintenance without disrupting the rest of the MCC.

Motor starter selection

The choice of starter directly impacts the starting current, available torque, energy efficiency and the useful life of the electrical installation.

DOL

Direct to line boot

Applies nominal voltage to the motor in a single step. Maximum starting torque, maximum simplicity. The impact on the network is severe: the inrush current can reach 8×In and generate voltage drops that affect other equipment.

I start6–8 × In Starting torque100% engine Typical power≤ 22 kW (480 V) StandardIEC 60947-4-1
Y-Δ

Star-delta

Starts in star (voltage reduced to 58%), then switches to delta at normal speed. Starting torque and current drop to 33% of DOL. Switching generates a transient that can exceed the DOL if load is present — requires load decoupling at startup.

I start2–2.7 × In Starting torque33% of engine Typical power15–200 kW ConditionNo-load start
Soft-Sterger

Electronic soft starter

Electronic control of starting voltage using thyristors. Allows you to configure the acceleration ramp and the limit current. Ideal for centrifugal pumps, compressors and loads with mechanical impact. Includes built-in phase imbalance and overtemperature protections.

I start2–4 × In (configurable) Starting torqueProgressive ramp Typical power7.5–630 kW BrandsABB, Siemens, Schneider
VFD

Variable frequency drive

Full control of motor speed, torque and frequency. Due to the affinity law (P∝n³), a 20% reduction in speed in pumps and fans reduces consumption by up to 49%. Controlled starting current ≤150% of In. It is the only solution when the process requires variable speed.

Industrial communication protocols

VMS Energy CCMs are integrated with the plant control system using leading industrial protocols. Selection depends on existing PLC/SCADA platform and determinism requirements.

Modbus RTU/TCP

The universal standard

Modbus RTU over RS-485 is the most widespread protocol in legacy industrial installations. Simple, robust and compatible with all PLCs and SCADA on the market. Modbus TCP moves the same data frame over Ethernet, eliminating the protocol gateway.

Reference regulatory framework

The rules presented below are of a nature information and represent the most common technical framework in industrial JCC projects. The regulations applicable to each project — including national standards, requirements of the client or competent authority — are determined and agreed at the basic engineering stage in accordance with the specific requirements of the scope contracted.

IEC 61439-1 Low voltage switchgear assemblies — General requirements. Defines design and routine tests, temperature limits and verification of the form of separation.
IEC 61439-2 Power sets. Specific extension for MCC: you define the cubicles, the form of separation (Form 1 to 4b) and the maintenance access requirements.
IEC 60947-4-1 AC motor starters. Regulates contactors, overload relays and motor protectors integrated in the MCC: employment categories, minimum short circuit current and operational life cycle.
NOM-001-SEDE-2012 Electrical installations — Article 430 (motors): overcurrent protection, sizing of supply conductors and protection of branch circuits for motors.
NFPA 70E Electrical safety in the workplace. VMS Energy conducts the arc flash study (Arc Flash) to determine the categories of PPE required by MCC maintenance personnel.
UL 508A Industrial control blobs. Required when the project involves export to the US or when the customer specifies UL certification for the complete board.

MCC EPC process with VMS Energy

From lifting to commissioning and FAT/SAT testing, VMS Energy manages the entire MCC cycle as a sole contractor.

1

Load lifting and scope definition

Complete inventory of motors (kW, voltage, service factor, duty cycle), environmental conditions of the electrical room (temperature, altitude, ATEX classified area), existing control platform and operational continuity requirements. Result: draft MCC technical specification.

2

Detail engineering: coordination of protections and Arc Flash

Calculation of symmetrical and asymmetric short circuit on each bus. Selection of MCCBs, contactors, thermal relays and motor protectors with Type 2 coordination (IEC 60947-4-1). NFPA 70E electric arc study: calculation of incident energy (cal/cm²) and determination of PPE category for each cubicle.

3

Factory FAT manufacturing and testing

Manufacturing of the MCC in a certified workshop. Factory acceptance tests (FAT) according to IEC 61439: point-to-point wiring verification, dielectric testing, insulation resistance measurement, verification of startup sequences with PLC simulator, and review of arc flash labels.

4

Installation, commissioning and commissioning

Mechanical assembly, power and control connection, parameter configuration of starters and drives, integration with the customer's communication protocol (Modbus, Profibus, EtherNet/IP, PROFINET) and communication verification with PLC/SCADA. Sequential start-up tests and process signal verification.

5

SAT tests and delivery of technical dossier

Site acceptance tests (SAT) with the client: verification of all control and protection functions under real operating conditions. Delivery of the complete technical dossier: final as-built drawings, preventive maintenance, recommended critical spare parts, FAT/SAT test reports and electric arc study.
