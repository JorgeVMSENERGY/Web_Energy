Industrial Electrical Reliability

When the network fails, its operation cannot fail.

VMS Energy designs, supplies and installs power backup and storage systems for critical industrial installations — from engineering to commissioning and maintenance.

Request free energy assessment Learn about our EPC process

Grid instability has a cost that doesn't appear on your bill.

Microcuts, voltage dips, frequency variations and prolonged interruptions are part of the industrial electrical operation in Mexico. The real impact is not measured in kilowatt-hours — it is measured in unplanned downtime, damaged equipment, lost production lots and unfulfilled contracts.

In sectors such as Oil & Gas, mining, food and beverage manufacturing, or energy generation, an outage of seconds can represent losses of hundreds of thousands of pesos and unacceptable security risks.

Before talking about technology, we ask three questions.

Not all backup solutions are created equal, and the wrong choice can mean costly oversizing or underprotection. At VMS Energy, the process begins with a diagnosis — not a catalog.

1

How long can you stop your process?

If the answer is zero milliseconds — you need an UPS. If the answer's minutes or hours — you need BESS. If the response is both — you need both working together.

2

What is the profile of your electrical consumption?

If you have peaks of demand that trigger your rate, variable charges, or a high demand charge bill — strategic storage (BESS) usually generates return in less than 4 years, calculated with your own receipt. If your charges are constant and critical — industrial UPS is the backbone of your continuity.

3

Do you have or plan to integrate solar generation?

If the answer is yes — storage turns your solar plant into a manageable, not intermittent source. The solar + BSS+network combination is the most efficient architecture for industry in Mexico today.

Four different needs. Only one person responsible for making them work.

VMS Energy is not a distributor. It is the integrator who diagnoses need, specifies the solution, installs it and sets it up under a single contract. What follows is not a catalogue: it is the criterion with which we decide what suits each installation.

Immediate critical support

There are loads that do not support even one interruption cycle: control rooms, instrumentation, security systems and emergency stop. For them, the transfer has to be imperceptible, and the quality of the energy delivered stable even if the network is not.

How we decided:
  • Which loads do not tolerate interruption and how long they should be sustained without network
  • What disturbances come today from the attack — gaps, transients, harmonic distortion
  • Site conditions: temperature, altitude, humidity, corrosion and required degree of protection
  • How the system should report to the SCADA or control center already installed

What we deliver: calculation memory, detail engineering, assembly, load testing and signed start-up protocol.

Energy demand and cost management

When the problem is not continuity but the bill, the objective changes: cut the peaks that set the demand charge and shift consumption outside of peak hours, without altering the production process.

How we decided:
  • Actual load profile measured on site — not estimated — contrasted with the contracted rate
  • How much of the bill corresponds to demand and how much to energy in each hourly period
  • What margin does the process leave: which loads can move and which cannot
  • Return calculated on the current rate and its update rule

What we deliver: feasibility study with figures of the receipt itself, engineering, installation and subsequent measurement confirming the savings.

Own generation integration

Photovoltaic generation is intermittent; the industrial process does not. Integrating it consists of converting a variable source into a manageable supply and leaving the interconnection in order.

How we decided:
  • Solar resource of the site and available useful surface, with its structural restrictions
  • Coincidence between the generation curve and the real consumption curve
  • Interconnection requirements and protections applicable to the connection point
  • If and how much to accompany the generation with storage

What we deliver: study of resource and incompetence, engineering of detail, civil and electrical works, interconnection and commissioning process.

Electrical support of the installation

Backup, demand management and own generation are installed on an internal network that must be prepared to receive them: voltage adaptation, coordinated protections, panels and pipelines that support the new operating regime.

How we decided:
  • Status of the existing installation: capacity, coordination of protections and grounding
  • What must be adapted before connecting and what can be kept
  • How the set behaves during the transfer and during the return to the network
  • What is documented so that maintenance can operate it without us

What we deliver: on-site survey, electrical studies, suitability, testing and closure file with plans of-146.

Security from within: the level of protection is defined in the project, not in the catalog.

Before talking about kWh, we talk about security. The following functions exist in the industrial market, but not all come in all systems nor act at the same level– Actual scope depends on selected equipment and project conditions. VMS Energy establishes this scope in the detailed engineering and verifies it in commissioning.

A storage system is a layered set: switchgear live within a pack, the Williams inside the cabinet, and the cabinet inside its installation. The question that decides a purchase is not how many security functions it comes with, but at what layer each one acts. — that is, at what distance from the origin it comes to intervene.

  • SwitchgearThe finest thermal sensing lives here. The more sensors per switchgear, the more time there is to react. 4
  • PackDeletion, smoke detector, sense and relief valve: The module behaves like a closed security unit. 1 · 3 · 4 · 5
  • CabinetOne set for the entire envelope: suppression, smoke and gas detection, and humidity and temperature control. 1 · 2 · 3 · 5
  • InstallationLocation, separations, ventilation and signed tests that verify that all of the above was as specified. 6
1

Automatic fire suppression

Agent that acts without water and without damaging the electronics, with thermal threshold triggering and without human intervention. Depending on the system, the agent is housed within each pack or in a single tank for the entire envelope.

Acts at pack or cabinet level
2

Humidity control and thermal regime

Maintains indoor relative humidity above the dew point on switchgear, BMS and power electronics. Condensation is a common cause of premature failure in humid weather and intermittent operation.

Acts at cabinet level
3

Early smoke detection

Detects combustion particles before the smoke is visible and launches the first level alarm that triggers the ordered shutdown. The closer the detector is to the origin, the sooner the warning arrives: that is why it matters if there is one per pack or one per cabinet.

Acts at pack or cabinet level
4

Thermal sensing and operating limits

Continuous temperature monitoring with alarm thresholds and automatic stoppage. Sensor density varies greatly between systems — from a few points per cabinet to several sensors per dozen switchgear — and it depends on how long there is to react.

Acts at the switchgear or pack level
5

Exhaust gas management

Gases released by a failing switchgear must be detected and evacuated before they accumulate. It is resolved with detection of combustible gas in the envelope, with unidirectional relief valves in each pack, or with both.

Acts at pack or cabinet level
6

FAT/SAT commissioning protocols

VMS Energy documentally reviews the acceptance tests in the factory and executes the acceptance tests on site: failure simulation, alarm activation, all sensor testing and protocol signing with the customer before delivery. It is the verification that the specified is what was installed.

Frequently asked questions

Technical answers on selection, sizing and integration of industrial UPS and BESS systems.

Which topology of Uzc is best for a critical industrial installation?

The norm IEC 62040-3 He ranks the UPS in three topologies. VFI (online double conversion) is the only one that provides total galvanic isolation and zero transfer time — it is the standard for Oil & Gas, mining and critical control rooms. VI (interactive) regulates voltage but bypasses during microcuts; suitable for less critical loads. VFD (standby) only acts when the network fails; valid for basic backup in offices. For industrial plants with continuous processes, VMS Energy exclusively specifies UPS VFI Borri E3001/E2001 because the load never sees the net directly.

How is the autonomy time of an industrial UPS calculated?

Autonomy depends on the actual power of the load (kW, not kVA — cosφ must be applied), the capacity of the battery bank (kWh) and the efficiency of the inverter (92–96% in modern VFI UPSs).

t (h) = C_banco (kWh) × η_inversor / P_carga (kW) Example: control room 30 kW, bank 72 kWh, η = 0.95 → autonomy = 72 × 0.95 / 30 = 2.28 hours

VMS Energy-Draft the bank according to the backup time required by the customer's business continuity plan.

What is the difference between LFP and VRLA/AGM batteries for an industrial BSS?

VRLA/AGM (sealed lead-acid): low initial cost, easy availability, but useful life of 3–5 years in frequent cycles, sensitive to temperature and low energy density (~30–50 Wh/kg). LFP (lithium ferrophosphate, LiFePO₄): ≥8000 life cycles, density 90–160 Wh/kg, operation from -20 °C to +60 °C, no memory effect, no maintenance. Our reference platform, Sigenergy SigenStack, uses 12 kWh LFP modules with security management at the pack level; Its UL 9540A and IEC 62619 tests are documented with Intertek. NFPA 855 is met in the design of the facility.

What is the "Cum" and how much can you save on the electric fare?

Peak shaving consists of downloading the BESS during demand peaks to reduce the maximum recorded in the CFE meter. In the GDMTH rate, demand charges (capacity and distribution) represent an important part of the bill, and the distribution charge varies greatly between divisions: in September 2026 it ranged from around 58 to 221 MXN/kW. A well-sized BESS can reduce the billed demand between 20% and 40%. With favorable profiles, the return is typically located below 4 years. We calculate it with your receipt and your measured load curve.

When should BESS be combined with solar generation and when should stand to be installed?

BESS standalone: when the objective is peak shaving, backup for CFE interruptions or frequency regulation. BESS + solar: optimal architecture when the plant has or plans photovoltaic panels — the BESS stores the midday solar surplus for use in the evening, eliminates curtailment and converts the installation into a semi-autonomous microgrid. With well-sized storage, self-consumption of solar energy increases significantly; We model it with its load curve before proposing capacity.

How does an UPS for data center differ from one for heavy industrial use?

Data center UPSs are designed for controlled environments (18–24°C) with stable electronic loads. Industrial UPS Borri E3001 operate in extreme conditions: -10 °C to +50 °C, HR up to 95%, corrosive gases, dust, engine starters and THDi elevated by variable frequency drives. They include galvanic isolation by integrated transformer, protection up to IP54 and certified construction for classified areas. Using a data center in industrial plant is the most frequent source of premature failures by condensation, corrosion and Overvoltage not attenuated.

How do we start an industrial project with the UPS or BESS with VMS Energy?

The process begins with an electrical survey: measurement of real load (kW and cosφ), recording of the demand curve in 15-minute windows for at least a week, inventory of critical vs non-critical loads and review of the history of interruptions. With this information we prepare the sizing engineering and present the technical-economic proposal with return analysis. Typical time for firm proposal: 5 to 10 business days. Contact us to schedule a free energy evaluation.

How much can a GMDTH-fare plant save using the BSS-based "Corporate" with the "Corto" D.R.D.?

The rate GDMTH (High Hourly Medium Voltage Demand, applicable to installations with 100 kW or more) includes charges for maximum demand recorded in 15-minute windows. With a BESS correctly sized for peak shaving, the plant unloads the system during peak consumption hours, reducing the peak demand billed by CFE.

Illustrative example: A plant with a maximum demand of 500 kW and a base of 320 kW can cut its peak with a BESS of 180 kW. The savings depend on its tariff division, how many hours the peak lasts and whether it happens in peak hours, because the capacity charge is not linear. That is why we calculate it with your receipt and not with an average. The typical return for profiles of this type is located below 4 years.

What does the BOS (Balance of System) of a BSS project include and who executes it?

The BOS (Balance of System) It includes all components external to the BESS equipment itself that are necessary for the system to operate safely, efficiently and connectedly. It does not include batteries, PCS or BMS — it includes everything that allows those equipment to function properly on site.

The BOS of an industrial BESS project includes: Electrical distribution (MV/LV switchgear, power switches), protection systems (protection coordination, SPD lightning rod), electric conductors (power wiring, trays, ducts), Civil works (foundation, electrical room or platform), maneuvers and installation (lifting, connection, testing), and grounding system (IEEE 80/IEC 60364 ground mesh). VMS Energy executes the complete BOS as part of the EPC scope — the customer receives the system ready to operate, not loose parts.

What permissions or regulations do you apply to install a BESS in Mexico?

The Mexican electrical system operates through four regulatory layers which interact with each other and affect the installation of an industrial BESS:

  • Planning and public policy (SENER): defines the priorities of the national electricity system and the storage guidelines.
  • Technical and administrative regulation (CNE + CFE): sets out the requirements for connecting, operating and maintaining electrical installations with generation or storage.
  • System operation (CENACE): ensures the balance between generation and demand in real time — relevant for BESS systems connected to the network in market share schemes.
  • Technical compliance on site (NOM-001-SEDE, NOM-029-STPS): electrical installations, security and fire protection on site.

An industrial BESS the-meter (behind the meter) is part of its load center: under the Electricity Sector Law it does not require a storage permit from the CNE. The technical installation standards do apply (NOM-001-SEDE, NOM-029-STPS, NOM-002-STPS) and, if it is integrated with its own generation, the interconnection contract or the corresponding permit depending on the capacity. VMS Energy manages technical and regulatory documentation as part of the EPC scope.
