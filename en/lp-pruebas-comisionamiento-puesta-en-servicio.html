Execution · Testing and commissioning

Testing, commissioning and commissioning: no equipment is energized without being tested

We plan, execute and document preoperative electrical tests, controlled energization and performance tests of transformers, panels and switchgear, MCC, medium voltage cables, protections, drives and generation systems. You receive the operating equipment and the dossier that proves it.

The problem

The most expensive time to discover a fault is the first power-up

At the end of a work the pressure is to start. It is fair when tests are skipped, “factory” settings are accepted and it is delivered without evidence. These are the situations we see the most:

Undetected shipping or assembly damage

A team that passed the test at the factory can arrive with moisture, a blow or a phoja connection. Without proof in place, the damage appears when energizing.

Protections with generic settings

Relays and switches that come out with factory values ​​and not with those of the coordination study: false trips or, worse, protections that do not trip.

Several suppliers, no one integrates

The board from one, the transformer from another and the drive from a third. Everyone tests their equipment; no one tests the entire system working together.

Delivery without evidence

Without signed protocols or reference values, the warranty is complicated and the maintenance area starts without a baseline against which to compare.

How we execute it

A sequence with two release points

Commissioning is not a test at the end: it is a sequence that begins before the equipment arrives on site. Nothing is energized without the release of preoperative testing, and nothing is delivered without accepted performance testing.

De-energized equipment Energized Equipment Release point: no progress without satisfactory results

Commissioning plan and protocols

We define what is tested, with what method, with what acceptance criteria and who testifies. It is remembered before the work, not during the start.

Test testing of tests, FAT

We attend factory acceptance tests of transformers, CCMs, disconnectors and drives to confirm that the equipment meets the data sheet before shipping.

Reception and inspection on site

Transport verification, data plate, visible damage and assembly and interconnection conditions before any electrical test.

Preoperative electrical testing (SAT)

With the equipment de-energized: insulation, transformation ratio, resistances, verification of control wiring, adjustments and tripping of protections, interlocks.

Release to energize

Controlled energization and commissioning

Energization in stages with programmed maneuvers, voltage verification, phase sequence and no-load behavior; configuration and startup of drives, generation and control.

Function and performance tests

Operation with real load: transfers, synchronization, control and communication loops with SCADA or DCS, and closing of pending (punch list) with the client.

Customer acceptance

The seventh stage, delivery to operation, is detailed in Deliverables. For the technical details of each test on transformers, consult our guides. FAT and SAT tests and Installation and commissioning of transformers.

Frequently asked questions

What difference is there between testing, commissioning and commissioning?
The tests verify the status of each piece of equipment, generally de-energized. Commissioning is the complete sequence that confirms that the system was installed and operates as designed, from test plan to performance testing. Commissioning is the energization and delivery to operation at the end of that sequence.
Can you commission equipment that you didn't supply or install?
Yes. We work in a multi-brand way and have put into service equipment from different manufacturers, supplied by third parties. In those cases we start with the reception and on-site inspection and define the protocol with the customer and the manufacturer's information.
Do you test factory tests for the FAT test?
Yes. We have participated in testing of transformers, engine control centers, 34.5 kV sectioners and frequency changers before your dispatch, as part of the supply or representing the customer.
What if a test does not meet the acceptance criteria?
The equipment is not released to energize. The result is documented, the cause is investigated with the manufacturer or installer, corrected, and the test is repeated. That's the reason for release points: to prevent a fault from being discovered upon energizing.
What do they deliver after the commission is over?
A T-shirt with plan and protocols, records of each test, settings applied in protections, variable frequency drives and control, the closed pending list and transfer to operation and maintenance personnel.
Do they work on offshore platforms and outside Jalisco and Campeche?
Yes. Much of our experience in testing and commissioning is on marine platforms of Campeche Probe, and we serve projects throughout Mexico, such as Tabasco, Veracruz, TON, Durango and the State of Mexico.

Do you have a boot at the door?

Tell us what equipment you are going to commission and what stage your project is at. We propose the testing and commissioning plan with scope, times and deliverables.
