Isolated microgrids

Microgrids for remote sites

Reliable energy where the grid does not reach: generation, solar and BESS under a single control

In a mine, a camp or a well far from the grid, generators do it all: covering base load, peaks and reserve, almost always at low load. Integrating solar and storage under common control reduces fuel, engine hours and supply trips, without risking the continuity of the operation.

Operate only on diesel

The most expensive liter is the one that burns at low load

A Threaded generation system for the start peak spends most of the day well below its capacity. That safety margin is paid every day in fuel, maintenance and logistics.

Low load motors

A group operating well below its rated power consumes more litres per kWh delivered and accumulates unburned fuel in the exhaust, forcing additional corrections and maintenance.

Fuel logistics

Pipes, roads, storage, losses and risk of shortages. In a remote location, each liter costs much more than its purchase price.

Engine hours

Each hour of operation brings the next service and the next overhaul closer. Operating more groups than necessary multiplies the maintenance cost.

A weak network

Starting a pump or large mill causes voltage and frequency drops that can trip other loads. Without coordinated control, the answer is to turn on more generators “just in case.”

How to integrate

Four layers operating as a single network

The generators provide the firm foundation, the solar provides the cheapest energy of the day, the BESS absorbs variations and starts and the EMS/PMS decides how many groups should be online at any given time.

Without replacing what already works

A microgrid can be built in stages

There is no need to replace the generation park. Each stage is paid for with what you save and prepare for the next.

Stage 1

Generation control

PMS with automatic start and stop according to demand, load distribution and ballast shedding by priority. Only the necessary groups operate, close to their efficient load.

Stage 2

Storage

The BESS takes over the rolling reserve and the engine starts, which allows turning off groups that today operate only "just in case."

Stage 3

Solar

With BESS and control in operation, solar is integrated with the penetration that the system supports, without compromising the stability of the site.

How we work

From fuel log to source loss test

1. Uprising

Measured load profile, fuel logs, cost per liter placed on site, group status, irradiation and space and access restrictions.

2. Modeling and sizing

Time-by-hour simulation of the dispatch, mixing of sources, study of the largest engine start, short circuit and coordination of protections.

3. Supply and installation

Procures multi-brand, equipment in container or skid when it is convenient to relocate it, assembly and integration with the existing generation without stopping the operation.

4. Testing and operation

Loss of a group, major load startup, ballast shedding and degraded mode, with signed protocols, training and maintenance.

Economy and regulatory framework

What defines savings and what the law requires

What defines savings

  • The real cost of the liter placed on site, with freight, storage and losses.
  • The hours of operation and the average load of each group.
  • The irradiation of the site and the shape of the load curve with respect to the solar day.
  • The years of life left in the operation, which define whether fixed or relocatable equipment is appropriate.

We calculate it with your logs and measurements, never with a generic percentage. Less fuel also means fewer emissions to report.

Current framework (Electrical Sector Law, 2025)

  • Isolated self-consumption is intended only for the site's own needs and does not require interconnection studies or registration in the electricity market.
  • With 0.7 MW or more of generation, a generation permit from the National Energy Commission (CNE) is required.
  • Machinery for renewable generation can be deducted 100% (art. 34, fr. XIII, LISR), with conditions. Validate your case with your tax advisor.

Frequently asked questions

How much fuel can be saved with a microgrid?
It depends on the irradiation of the site, the load profile, how much solar energy the system can absorb and the price of the fuel put on site. We do not give a percentage without data: we calculate it with an hour-by-hour simulation from your fuel logs and load measurements.
Do I have to replace my generators?
Normally not. Existing groups are integrated if their control supports parallel operation and communication with the PMS; if not, the control (governor, voltage regulator, or group controller) is updated. We work with DEIF, Woodward and Schneider Electric platforms.
What if the BESS or the control system fails?
The microgrid is designed with a degraded mode: if the BESS or EMS goes out of service, the generators continue to operate with their local control and load sharing between them. Fuel optimization is lost, not site energy.
Can the microgrid be relocated when the site operation is complete?
Yes, if designed for it. Generation, BESS and boards can be supplied in containers or patins, and the solar in fast-mount structures, to move to another work front or to another mining unit.
Does an isolated microgrid need permission from the CNE?
With 0.7 MW or more of generation, isolated self-consumption requires a generation permit from the CNE, although it does not require interconnection studies or registration in the electricity market. We review the procedure applicable to each project.

We review your fuel logs and load profile and tell you how much of your generation can be covered by solar and storage.
