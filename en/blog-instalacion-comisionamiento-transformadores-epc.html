Installation and commissioning of transformers in EPC projects: from transportation to the first kilowatt

The complete protocol — from site receipt to operational delivery — to ensure that no transformer is energized before it is ready.

In an EPC project, the installation and commissioning of an industrial transformer is not simply about downloading the equipment and connecting it. It is a sequence of interdependent technical steps where an error at any stage can mean anything from a costly delay to an irreversible catastrophic failure. Each step has a specific purpose and each verification exists because someone, on some project, did not do it and paid the consequences.

This article documents the protocol that VMS Energy executes in each transformer commissioning—from the coordination call before dispatch to the delivery of the commissioning dossier to the customer—, with the level of detail that a field engineer needs to execute or supervise it.

1. Reception on site and transportation verification

Reception does not begin when the truck arrives at the site: it begins with logistical planning. For transformers of more than 5 MVA or with dimensions that exceed the free passage on restricted routes, permits for the transportation of oversized cargo must be processed in advance. The commissioning engineer must know the weight, dimensions and discharge point prior to arrival.

Critical checks at the time of download

  • Shock accelerometer (shock indicator): The manufacturer installs an impact tag on the tank. If the indicator shows activation (color change), the device received a shock above the programmed threshold (typically 2–3 g). Do not move equipment to the installation site without documenting and notifying the manufacturer and insurance.
  • Positive nitrogen pressure: Oil-filled transformers are shipped with the tank filled with slightly positive pressure nitrogen (0.3–0.5 psi). The pressure is checked with the conservator's manometer. Negative pressure indicates that air (and possibly moisture) entered the tank during transportation.
  • Exterior visual inspection: Look for tank deformations, leaks in seals and flanges, bent bushings, damage to radiators or terminal boxes. Photograph any anomalies before moving it.
  • Plate documentation: Verify that the unit received corresponds exactly to the order: power, voltage ratio, vector group, Zcc, and FIND code. A transformer exchanged in factory or logistics is more common than it seems.
If accelerometer is enabled: Do not energize under any circumstances until a complete diagnosis is carried out (TTR, winding resistance, tangent delta, DGA). Repair of a transformer that failed due to undetected shipping damage is the responsibility of the customer and contractor, not the manufacturer.

2. Preparation of the area and civil works

The transformer cannot be installed on any surface. The civil works of the substation must be completed and validated before positioning the equipment. The minimum elements are:

Foundation and base

Level and load

The concrete base must be flat (maximum deviation: 3 mm in 3 m), with a load capacity greater than the total weight of the transformer with oil. The positioning brackets or rails must be anchored according to civil plans.

Containment bucket

Environmental protection

Mandatory for oil transformers. The bucket must contain 110% of the total volume of transformer oil. Without a finished and approved cube, the transformer is not installed. NOM-138-SEMARNAT applies.

Land system

Equipotential mesh

The transformer tank ground point must be connected to the substation ground grid before any other connection. Check mesh resistance according to NOM-001-SEDE-2012.

Access and barriers

Perimeter security

The area must be fenced, marked with high voltage warnings and with controlled access before any wiring is started. Verify that civil works personnel have evacuated the area.

3. Positioning and lifting

The lifting of an industrial transformer is a critical operation that requires specific planning, certified equipment and competent personnel. It is not improvised in the field.

Lifting plan requirements

  • Lifting points: Use only the hoist hooks or hoists marked on the tank by the manufacturer. Never hold from pistes, radiators or accessories.
  • Crane capacity: The crane must have a minimum capacity of 125% of the weight to be lifted, considering the actual angle of the cables and the operating radius.
  • Maximum inclination: During lifting and unloading, the transformer should not be inclined more than 15° from the horizontal plane (or as indicated by the manufacturer in the manual). Excessive tilt on an oil-filled transformer can displace the oil and uncover parts of the core, causing local oxidation.
  • Final positioning: The wheel axle must be aligned with the base guides according to the plan; oil drain plugs must be left accessible for future maintenance; the radiators oriented towards where they allow natural convinction if it is ONAN.
EPC Note: In projects where the transformer will be installed in a closed area (transformer room), the lifting and introduction sequence must be coordinated with the civil works schedule. Once the structure is closed, moving equipment may be impossible without demolishing parts of the building.

4. Oil filling and accessories connection

Distribution transformers smaller than 1 MVA usually arrive filled with oil from the factory. Those with greater power frequently arrive without oil (or with partial oil) to reduce weight in transportation. On-site filling is a delicate operation that, if done incorrectly, can contaminate the oil with moisture.

On-site filling procedure

  1. Verify that the new oil has quality sheet: Dielectric strength ≥ 60 kV/mm, humidity < 10 ppm, acidity < 0.05 mgKOH/g before filling.
  2. Empty the nitrogen from the tank slowly by the designated valve, through a deacasting filter to prevent wet air from entering.
  3. Vacuum filling: The preferred method is to fill the transformer under vacuum (< 1 mbar) to ensure that no air bubbles remain trapped. The oil enters through the bottom; The vacuum is applied from the top.
  4. Mandatory rest: Once filled, wait at least 12 hours (24 hours for HV) before the first energize to allow the oil to completely impregnate the paper and the residual bubbles escape.
  5. Check oil final level in the indicator of the conservative at reference temperature (25°C). Adjust if necessary.

Accessory connection

Before electrical connection, check and connect all transformer accessories:

  • Buchholz relay (in line between tank and conservator) — verify correct orientation of the flow arrow
  • Oil thermometer and devanate temperature transducer (OTI/WTI)
  • Oil level indicator
  • Oil sampling valves (HV and LV if applicable)
  • Manual or motorized tap changer (check initial position)
  • Oil fans and pumps if the Fever code is ONAF or OFAF

5. HV and LV connection — sequence and verifications

The electrical connection of the transformer is the step with the greatest risk for personnel if the correct verifications are not carried out. The correct sequence minimizes the risk of accident and polarity errors.

Recommended connection sequence

1

Tank ground connection

First connection made, last connection removed. The transformer tank must be equipotentially connected to the substation ground grid before touching any terminal. Check connection resistance with a micro-ohmmeter (< 1 Ω).

2

Connection of the LV neutral to ground

In Dyn11 configuration, the secondary neutral is connected solidly to ground (TN system) or through neutral resistance (IT or TT system) according to the coordination study. Never leave the neutral without a defined connection.

3

LV cable connection

Connect the secondary (LV) cables to the transformer terminal blocks, verifying the phase sequence according to the single-wire plane: A-B-C in the correct order for the downstream distribution board. Retighten to the manufacturer's specified torque.

4

HV cable connection

The high voltage cables are connected last, with the HV switch in the open and locked position. Verify minimum insulation distances in air according to NOM-001-SEDE-2012 and approved plans. The torque of the HV connectors follows the bushing manufacturer's specifications.

5

Protection and alarm wiring

Control wiring connection: Buchholz relay contacts (alarm and trip), OTI and WTI overtemperature contacts, oil level indicator, and OLTC alarms if applicable. Check continuity and polarity of each control circuit.

Absolute rule: No worker should be in physical contact with transformer terminals or conductors while there is a possibility that the HV switch can be operated—even if it is supposedly open. Apply certified LOTO (Lockout-Tagout) on the HV switch throughout the connection process.

6. Pre-energization tests (cold commissioning)

Cold commissioning verifies that the transformer and its auxiliary system are ready to be energized. It is carried out with the HV switch open and the transformer without voltage in any part of the equipment.

TestMethodAcceptance criteria
Transformation Ratio (TTR) TTR instrument on all taps Deviation ≤ 0.5% from nominal
Vector group verification TTR vector group mode or manual test Plate conformal vector group (e.g. Dyn11)
Winding resistance Micro-ohmmeter, DC current Imbalance < 2%; variation vs. FAT < 5%
Insulation resistance (Megger) Depending on voltage level (500–5,000 V DC) PI > 2.0; RA > 1,000 MΩ at test temperature
Oil analysis (stiffness and humidity) Cup test IEC 60156; Karl Fischer BDV ≥ 40 kV/mm (MV); H₂O < 20 ppm
Protection and alarm verification Functional test of Buchholz contacts, OTI, WTI All operational and verified contacts
LOTO test and barriers Visual verification and temporary landing continuity test All temporary landings withdrawn; LOT removed only by authorized responsible
Taps changer check Manual or motorized operation on all taps Smooth operation; nominal tap position for first energization

All tests should be documented with numerical results before proceeding. The signature of the responsible engineer in the SAT protocol is the guarantee that the equipment is ready for the first energization.

7. First energization — step-by-step protocol

The first energization is the moment of greatest uncertainty in commissioning. All possible cold checks have been completed, but the actual behavior of the equipment under voltage is only observable at this moment. The protocol must guarantee that if something fails, the consequences are as minimal as possible.

1

Coordination with the network operator

Notify the HV network operator (CFE or private operator) of the intention to energize. Confirm the current voltage and frequency at the connection point. Obtain formal authorization if the project requires it.

2

Personnel-free zone verification

Confirm that no worker is within the danger area of the transformer and the HV switchgear. Verify that all switchgear doors are closed. The commissioner must be in a position with direct line of sight to the control panel and the transformer.

3

Energization of the HV switchgear without load in the secondary

The LV switch or the main switch on the downstream switchboard must be in the OPEN position. Close the HV switch. The transformer remains energized in vacuum. Immediately check for the absence of abnormal noises, vibrations, sparks or any alarm indication. If something is abnormal: open the HV switch immediately.

4

No-load voltage measurement

With the transformer energized in vacuum, measure the voltages at the secondary (LV) terminals: phase voltages, neutral-to-ground voltage, and verify the phase sequence with a sequencemeter. The values ​​must correspond to the nominal secondary voltage ± the selected tap setting.

5

Load connection — progressive load

Once the voltages are confirmed, connect the main switch to the board. If the system allows, charge progressively (25% → 50% → 75% → 100%) monitoring phase currents, voltage, temperature and any alarm indication.

6

Recording of measurements under initial load

At the first stable load: record currents of each phase (HV and LV), line and phase voltages, power factor, oil temperature and ambient temperature. This record is the baseline of the equipment in operation.

8. Commissioning under load — first 72 hours

The first 72 hours of operation are the period of greatest surveillance. Most post-energization faults that are not detectable when cold manifest themselves in this interval.

TimeVerificationAction if there is anomaly
0–1 hour Complete visual inspection: oil level, noise, vibration, odor. Measurement of phase currents. Any anomaly: de-energize and evaluate
4 hours Oil temperature (OTI) in correct trend. Verify that ONAF fans start at the programmed threshold. Higher than expected temperature for loading: check radiator obstruction
24 hours Complete measurement: voltages, currents, oil and winding temperature, FP. DGA sample #1 (first post-energization sample). Acetylene in DGA: de-energize immediately
48 hours Visual inspection of terminal blocks with portable IR camera (with charge). Check adjustment of covers and flanges. Hot spot ΔT > 15°C: retighten or investigate
72 hours Final record of measurements under static load. DGA sample #2 and comparison with sample #1. Explosive increase in any gas: report and evaluate
Early release criteria (early release): If the 24-hour DGA does not show acetylene and all gases are below the Condition 1 thresholds (IEEE C57.104), and all electrical measurements are normal, the transformer can be delivered to the customer with the DGA at 72 hours as confirmation. In critical projects, VMS Energy recommends a third DGA at 30 days as the closing of the commissioning.

9. Documentation and delivery to the client

The commissioning dossier is the final deliverable of this phase of the EPC project. It is not an administrative procedure: it is the legal and technical record that defines the reference status of the equipment, protects the contractor in case of disputes and is the starting point of the maintenance program.

Contents of the VMS Energy commissioning dossier

  • Reception report with photographs and accelerometer status
  • Complete SAT protocol: TTR, winding resistance, Megger + PI, vector group
  • Pre-energization oil analysis (stiffness, humidity, basal DGA)
  • First energization record: date, time, network conditions, personnel present
  • No-load measurement recording: phase voltages, phase sequence
  • Record of measurements under load at 4 hours, 24 hours, 48 hours and 72 hours
  • DGA at 24 hours and 72 hours (for oil-filled transformers)
  • Termography IR deneeras HV and LV at 48 h
  • Punch list (list of minor deviations and their resolution)
  • As-built drawings of the final connection
  • Transformer Manufacturer's Manual and Data Sheet
  • Recommendations for the initial predictive maintenance program

10. Case study: 5 MVA substation, Guanajuato automotive plant

EPC Case — Automotive Plant, Silao, Guanajuato

Power transformer 5 MVA, 34.5 kV / 13.8 kV / 480 V, Din11–Dyn11, ONAF — Triff transformer of two secondary windings

Context: EPC project for new assembly line. 5 MVA power transformer with two independent secondaries: one of 13.8 kV for internal distribution to MV panels, and one of 480 V for the assembly line. The team arrived with the tank pressurized with nitrogen, without oil, to be filled on site. Empty weight: 14,200 kg; total weight with oil: 21,800 kg. The commissioning was coordinated with the plant production stoppage: 6-day window.

Schedule executed:

  • Day 1: Reception. Accelerometer: not activated. N₂ pressure: 0.42 psi — correct. Visual inspection without anomalies. The 35-ton crane performed the positioning in 4 hours without incident.
  • Days 1–2: Vacuum oil filling (7,600 liters of new oil, BDV = 68 kV/mm, 6 ppm H₂O). Rest period of 24 hours.
  • Day 3: HV connection (34.5 kV), MV and LV secondaries, neutrals and ground. Complete protective wiring.
  • Day 4: Cold commissioning (SAT). TTR on all three windings: max deviation 0.09%. Dyn11–Dyn11 vector group confirmed. Megger: PI HV = 3.8, PI BT¹ = 3.2, PI BT² = 3.5. Post-filling stiffness: 61 kV/mm. Basal DGA: all < 3 ppm, acetylene: ND. Everything agrees.
  • Day 5: First energization at 07:30 hrs. Vacuum energization: no abnormal noises. Voltages in 480 V LV winding: 481–480–481 V (within ±0.5% of nominal tap). MV charging connection first to 30%, then to 100% in 2 hours. Measurements at the time: balanced currents, oil temperature 28°C above ambient, ONAF fans started at 58°C according to setpoint. DGA at 24 hours: without acetylene, TDCG = 45 ppm — Condition 1.
  • Day 6: Thermography with 85% load: ΔT maximum enbreaders = 4°C, within the normal range. DGA at 72 h: TDCG = 52 ppm, without significant variation. Plant in production starter. Delivery of commissioning wire to the customer.

Result: Commissioning completed in 6 days (within shutdown window). Equipment delivered with all metrics within criteria. DGA follow-up scheduled at 30 days and 6 months.

Frequently asked questions about installation and commissioning

How long does the commissioning of a distribution transformer take?

Between 3 and 7 business days for transformers up to 5 MVA, including reception, positioning, connection, SAT and first energization. In power transformers with in-situ oil filling and vacuum drying, the process can be extended to 2–4 weeks. The commissioning window must be defined in the EPC schedule from the engineering phase.

What risk does energize without full SAT involve?

Serious and irreversible risks: hidden transport damage manifesting as catastrophic failure in the first minutes; incorrect vector group polarity destroying parallel equipment; or oil with insufficient rigidity that causes internal discharge when voltage is applied. The SAT is the last barrier before exposing the equipment, personnel and process to unacceptable risks.

Why is it necessary to wait before the first energyization when filling with oil in place?

The oil needs at least 12–24 hours to completely impregnate the insulation paper and for trapped air bubbles to escape to the surface. Energizing before this process is complete involves the risk of partial discharge in the bubbles, which can progressively damage the insulation or cause immediate failure.

How is the vector group verified before energizing?

With TTR instrument in vector group mode, or manually injecting low magnitude voltage into the primary and measuring voltages between combinations of secondary terminals. It is critical when the transformer will be connected in parallel with another existing one: both must have the same vector group or the circulating currents when synchronizing will destroy both equipment.

What documents does the EPC contractor deliver at the end?

The commissioning dossier includes: reception report, complete SAT protocol, pre-energization oil analysis, first energization record, record of measurements under load (4h, 24h, 48h, 72h), DGA at 24h and 72h, IR thermography, resolved punch list, as-built drawings and predictive maintenance recommendations.

Is it necessary to install protections before the first energyization?

Yes, absolutely. At the very least they must be active: the HV switch with over-intensity protection, the Buchholz reliever (in oil transformers), the OTI and WTI temperature protections, and the differential protection if the project contemplates it. To enumerate without BuchholZ active is especially risky: the first internal defect produces a failure of greater magnitude before any other protection of the environment.

What's the difference between cold and hot commissioning?

Cold commissioning verifies the equipment without energizing the HV part: SAT, protection wiring verification, functional test of the Buchholz and the OLTC. Hot commissioning is the sequence of first energization and progressive charging. Both stages are mandatory; The cold does not replace the SAT, and the hot cannot be done without having completed the cold.
