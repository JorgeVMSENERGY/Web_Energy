How to decide

Types of medium voltage switchgear

A medium voltage switchgear is the assembly of aparamenta — switch, seccionators, fuses, protections and measurement — housed in a metal envelope to control and protect circuits of more than 1 kV and up to 36 kV (IEC classification). They are chosen by two axes.

1 Degree of compartmentalization: how much can be maneuvered with the panel energized

2 Insulating medium: air, gas or hybrid

Air-insulated (AIS)

Air at atmospheric pressure; normally vacuum switch.

  • Dust, moisture and salinity tolerance
  • Access for on-site maintenance

The most common in industry. Simple and with accessible components, in exchange for more surface area.

Hybrid or compact

Gas bars or switch; the rest in air.

Midpoint. It takes up less space than an AIS without reaching the cost of a complete GIS.

Gas insulated (GIS)

Active parts in sealed tank with SF6 or another gas.

  • Dust, moisture and salinity tolerance
  • Access for on-site maintenance

Platforms, coast, mines and small rooms. Requires monitoring gas pressure; An internal fault is repaired at the factory.

What's inside

Components and applications of a medium voltage switchgear

What is specified in each section depends on its function: connection, link, feeder, transformer protection or measurement.

The switchgear of a feeder section

  1. 1
    Power switch. Opens and closes under load and interrupts overcurrents and short circuits. Vacuum or SF6, fixed or removable.
  2. 2
    Disconnector and ground blade. They isolate a part of the system and ground it to work safely. They do not interrupt fault current.
  3. 3
    Limiting fuses. They protect against overcurrent, especially in branches to transformers and in sectional switchgear.
  4. 4
    Protection relays. They measure current and voltage and order the trip: phase and ground overcurrent, differential, under and over voltage. Its adjustment comes from the coordination study.
  5. 5
    Instrument transformers (TC and TP). They reduce current and voltage to values ​​that relays and meters read.
  6. 6
    Bars, interlocks and control. The bars distribute the energy between sections; Interlocks prevent maneuvers in incorrect sequence.

Frequently asked questions

What they ask us before quoting

What is a medium-voltage switchgear?
It is a metal enclosure that houses the switchgear to operate, protect and measure a circuit of more than 1 kV and up to 36 kV: switches, disconnectors, fuses, protection relays and instrument transformers. Several switchgear coupled by bars form a medium voltage distribution board.
What is the difference between an isolated air switchgear (AIS) and a gas isolated switchgear (GIS)?
AIS uses air as an insulator: it is cheaper and more accessible for maintenance, but it takes up more space. The GIS encloses the active parts in gas (typically SF6): it is compact and immune to dust, humidity and salinity, but costs more and requires monitoring the gas pressure. GIS is chosen when space or environment requires it; in the rest of the cases, AIS.
Should the switchgear be rehabilitated or the board replaced?
It depends on where the wear is. If the envelope, the bars and the compartments are healthy and what failed is the switch or a specific section, replacing that part is faster and avoids re-engineering of civil works and pipelines. If the switchgear no longer has spare parts or the short circuit level of the installation has grown above the capacity of the panel, replacing is the answer. The short circuit study is what decides, not the age of the equipment.
What is needed to quote a switchgear?
At a minimum: nominal voltage, nominal bus current, short-circuit current available at that point, number and function of the sections, and whether the switchgear is installed indoors or outdoors. With the one-line diagram and the short circuit study, the quote comes out without assumptions.
Is the difference between metal-clad and metal-en-ln-ln is only price?
No. Metal-clad separates the bars, switch, cables and control into grounded metal compartments, and has a removable switch with shutters. This is what allows one section to be maneuvered and maintained with the rest of the deck in service. Metal-enclosed protects against direct contact but without that complete separation. The decision is made for operational continuity, not cost.
Do they put into service or just supply?
Both things, and also separately. VMS Energy executes the complete scope—specification, supply, assembly, testing and energization—or enters only the part that is needed, which is usual in rehabilitations on panels that are already installed.

Do you have the short-circuit studio for that substation?

It is the document that decides whether the switchgear you have installed is still adequate, and the one that avoids quoting based on assumptions. If you don't have it, we make it.
