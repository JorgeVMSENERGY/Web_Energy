Electrical Availability Architecture

Industrial microgrids: grid, on-site generation, UPS, BESS, solar and control in a single system

A plant is not run out of power due to lack of equipment, but because each equipment was purchased separately. We design the back layers as a system: what load each protects, in what order they act when the network fails and who coordinates them.

The complete architecture

Six layers, one operation logic

Each layer solves a different problem. The grid supplies, on-site generation provides autonomy, the UPS covers the first milliseconds, the BESS covers minutes or hours and reduces the bill, solar lowers the cost of energy and the EMS/PMS decides what goes in, what goes out and in what order.

When the layers are controlled together, the plant operates as a microgrid

An industrial microgrid is this same system with an additional condition: it can be separated from the network in a controlled way and continue to operate with its own sources. Three elements define it: local generation and storage, a common control (EMS/PMS) and an interconnection point that opens and closes with sync and protections designed for both states.

Mode 1

Connected to the network

Operates in parallel with CFE. The BESS cuts demand peaks, the solar reduces grid consumption and the EMS limits injection when the interconnection does not support export. The generator remains in reserve.

Mode 2

On island

In the event of a failure, or on a scheduled basis during service maintenance, the microgrid is separated from the network. The BESS or the generator forms the internal network, the EMS de-ballasts by priority and the return is synchronized, without a second stoppage.

Mode 3

Isolated, without network

In mines, camps, pumping or platforms where the grid does not reach or is unstable, the microgrid is the complete electrical system: thermal, solar and BESS generation coordinated to reduce fuel. Microgrids for remote sites

When the network fails

The sequence we designed and tested

A well integrated architecture responds in a predictable order. The times indicated are typical ranges; the real values are fixed in engineering and verified in the acceptance tests on site.

  1. t = 0

    Network fails

    The relays detect loss of voltage or frequency out of range and the installation is separated from the network so as not to feed the fault.

  2. 0ms

    The UPS supports the critical

    Control, instrumentation, emergency stop systems and IT remain powered without interruption from the UPS battery bank.

  3. more to s

    The BESS takes essential loads

    If the system is configured to operate on the island, the BESS forms the internal network and feeds essential loads while starting the generation.

  4. - 10–30 s

    Generator starts and transfers

    The group starts, stabilizes voltage and frequency and the transfer connects it. With multiple groups, the controller synchronizes them and distributes the load.

  5. Continuous

    EMS/PMS de-ballasts and manages

    If the available capacity is not sufficient, the system disconnects non-essential loads by priority and reconnects them when there is room.

  6. return

    Orderly return to the network

    When the network normalizes, the transfer returns the load (open, closed or soft, depending on design), the generator cools down and the BESS recharges.

Life safety loads (for example, fire protection systems or evacuation lighting) have their own regulatory requirements: they are defined with the current NOM-001-SEDE and the standards applicable to the site, not only with cost criteria.

Control / EMS / PMS

The layer that converts loose equipment into a system

An energy management system (EMS) or power management system (PMS) decides in real time which source feeds what charge. We specify, program and integrate it with platforms from DEIF, Woodward and Schneider Electric, depending on the scale and existing infrastructure of the site.

Startup, synchronization and load distribution

Automatic start and stop of groups, synchronization between machines and with the network, active and reactive load distribution and N+1 redundancy.

Load shedding

Priority disconnection due to lack of capacity, low frequency or loss of a source, with staggered reconnection to avoid causing a second trip.

Demand management

Peak shaving with BESS, off-peak consumption shifting and zero injection control when the interconnection does not support export.

SCADA integration

Communication with the plant SCADA or DCS through industrial protocols (Modbus, Profibus, industrial Ethernet), alarms and event recording for subsequent analysis.

Applicable experience: engineering of the load segregation system of the Akal-J process center (Diavaz), synchronization of five Wärtsilä 3,000 kW motor generators at the P.S.S. Chemul and automatic transfer boards integrated to SCADA on the Nohoch-A2 platform.

Economy and regulatory framework

What the bill changes and what the law requires

GDMTH invoice

In the GDMTH tariff (medium voltage, 100 kW or more) demand charges—capacity and distribution—represent an important part of the bill, and the price of peak energy can be several times that of the base hour. In September 2026, the distribution charge varies considerably between CFE divisions.

A well-Shaped BESS attacks both components. With favorable profiles, the return is typically located below four years. We confirm it with the receipt and load curve measured from each plant, never with an average.

Current framework (Electrical Sector Law, 2025)

  • The National Energy Commission (CNE) replaced the CRE as regulator.
  • Generation less than 0.7 MW: distributed generation, with interconnection contract and without permission from the CNE.
  • Self-consumption of 0.7 MW or more: generation permit required; between 0.7 and 20 MW a simplified procedure applies.
  • Storage of 0.7 MW or more not associated with a power plant or load center requires a storage permit.
  • Isolated self-consumption (microgrid without connection to the grid) of 0.7 MW or more: requires generation permit, without interconnection studies or market registration (DACG of self-consumption, 2025).
  • Machinery for renewable generation or efficient cogeneration can be deducted 100% (art. 34, fr. XIII, LISR), with conditions. Validate your case with your tax advisor.

Frequently asked questions

What is an industrial micro-grid and how is it different from a traditional back?
A microgrid is a set of local sources (generators, Bss, solar) and loads under a common control, capable of operating connected to or separated from the network in a controlled manner. A traditional backup only starts when the network fails; in a microgrid the EMS/PMS manages the sources all the time, also to reduce the bill.
Can I turn my current installation into a microgrid?
In many cases yes. Existing generators, UPS and solar systems can be integrated by adding EMS/PMS control and, where warranted, a BESS. Before we check if the inverters and controllers support island operation and we recalculate protections, because in island the available short circuit current is lower than with the grid.
The BESS replaces the generator?
Depends on the autonomy required. For backups of minutes to a few hours, a BESS can cover essential loads without fuel. For long or uncertain autonomy, the generator is still necessary; In that case, the BESS reduces starts, hours of operation and engine consumption, and covers the seconds that the generator takes to take charge.
If I already have an U.S.P., do I need a
Not necessarily. The UPS protects specific critical loads with short autonomy. A BESS is justified when you need support for larger loads, greater autonomy or when its rate and demand profile allow you to recover the investment with peak shaving.
My solar panels back me up in a blackout?
Not by themselves. An interconnected photovoltaic system is disconnected when the grid fails for safety. For solar to continue contributing during an interruption, storage and a system capable of forming an island network, with the corresponding protections, are required.
What brands are they?
We are multi-brand integrators. In generation control we work with platforms from DEIF, Woodward and Schneider Electric; in storage our reference platform is Sigenergy. The final selection depends on the diagnosis, scale and infrastructure that already exists in your plant.
Do I need permission to install a BESS?
Under the Electricity Sector Law, storage of 0.7 MW or more that is not associated with a power plant or a load center requires permission from the CNE. A BESS installed behind your plant meter is treated as part of your load center; We review in each project what procedures and technical standards apply.
Does a microgrid need permission from the CNE?
The microgrid is not a separate regulatory figure: the generation and storage capacity and whether it is interconnected count. With 0.7 MW or more of generation, permission from the CNE is required, also for isolated self-consumption, which does not require interconnection studies. We review the procedure applicable to each project.
