Power factor correction solutions

The correct solution depends on the load profile, the presence of harmonics, the voltage level and the target cosφ.

Basic

Fixed capacitor bank

One or more stages of the bus permanently connected to the bus. Ideal for constant reactive loads (always on motors, furnaces, high power transformers). Easy installation and low maintenance cost.

Stable chargingFixed MVARIEC 60831Low cost
Industry standard

APFC Automatic Bank

Controller measures PF in real time and switches steps of capacitors (thyristors or contactors) to maintain target cosφ. Supports variable loads (production lines, machinery with cycles). From 50 to 5,000 kVAr in configurable steps.

Variable loadPFR ControllerThyristors/contactorsIEC 60831
Networks with harmonics

Detuned bank with reactors

Reactor in series with each step of capacitors, tuned so that the resonance frequency is below the 5th harmonic. Prevents capacitors from amplifying harmonic distortion in networks with VFDs, UPS or rectifiers. Typical detuning: 7%, 5.67% or 14% depending on network.

VFD/retifiersSeries reactorIEC 61921Antiharmonic
Power quality

Active Harmonic Filter (APF)

Four-quadrant inverter that injects compensation currents in real time, canceling harmonics generated by non-linear loads. Achieves THDi <5% (or <3%) and corrects PF simultaneously. Optimal solution when analysis shows THDi >20% or when IEEE 519 is contractual.

THDi <5%IEEE 519FP and harmonicsReal time
Hybrid

APFC + hybrid active filter

The APFC bank provides most of the reactive MVAR at low cost, while the active filter handles the dynamic residual and harmonics. Best cost-benefit ratio for medium-large plants with mixed linear and non-linear loads.

Regulatory framework

Reference regulatory framework

The standards presented are of a information and represent the most common technical framework for power factor correction projects. The regulations applicable to each project — including national standards, customer or competent authority requirements — are determined and agreed at the basic engineering stage in accordance with the specific requirements of the contracted scope.

IEC 60831-1/2

LV power capacitors. Defines rated voltage, admissible overvoltage, dielectric strength, temperature and type and routine tests for capacitors < 1 kV.

IEC 60871-1

MV power capacitors (≥ 1 kV). For medium voltage banks: 6–34.5 kV. Defines admissible overvoltage (1.10×Uc continuous), reactive current and protection coordination.

IEC 61921

Power capacitor banks. Applies to complete assemblies (bench + reactor + protections). Design requirements, protection coordination and verification for installation.

IEC 61000-3-12

Harmonics > 16 A per phase. Limits of THDi and individual harmonic currents in the PCC. Applies when the bank or load exceeds 16 A per phase in LV.

IEEE 519-2022

ANSI/IEEE harmonics standard. Reference for projects with NFPA/NEC codes. Defines THDv ≤ 5% and THDi limits according to the Icc/IL ratio in the PCC.

CFE GDMTH-2022

Quality of CFE supply. Establishes the allowable voltage, frequency and form factor ranges. Basis for determining whether the customer's network meets the required quality before installing the solution.

Frequently asked questions

Common doubts about power factor

When does the CFE penalty apply for power factor?
CFE applies a penalty when the monthly average power factor falls below 0.90 in the medium voltage rates (HM, HS, H-Sh). The additional charge may reach 3% for each percentage point below 0.90. For example, a 0.80 FP can generate a surcharge of up to 30% on billable demand. The penalty is shown in the "Power Factor" line of the invoice and applies to the maximum demand charge.
What is the difference between a fixed bank and an automatic bank?
A fixed bank injects a constant amount of MVAR into the network. It is suitable when reactive charge is stable (engines running at constant charge, furnaces). Automatic bench (APFC) uses a controller that measures the FP in real time and connects or disconnects steps from the TUNES to keep the FP target (typically 0.95–0.98 inductive), even when the load varies during the day or week. The cost of the automatic bank is higher, but eliminates the risk of overcompensation (training VET, which also penalizes CFE).
Why is the bench needed dettonized in networks with harmonics?
In networks with variable frequency drives, UPS or switching power supplies, current harmonics can create parallel resonance with standard UMENTS, amplifying harmonic stresses and destroying capacitors in a few weeks. detonated bench It incorporates calibrated serial reactors so that the resonance frequency is below the 5th harmonic (300 Hz in 60 Hz system). The most common de-intonation is 7% (p=7) or 5.67% (p =5.67), depending on the dominant Ironic order.
What advantage does an active harmonica filter have over a bank in the United States?
A active filter (APF) Injects real-time compensation currents to cancel harmonica generated by non-linear loads, achieving THDi <5% or even <3%. In addition, it can simultaneously correct the power factor and dynamically respond to charge changes in microsecond cycles. The disadvantage is its higher cost (2–4× compared to an equivalent APFC bank). It is the preferred solution when the THDi exceed 20% or when the IEEE 519 standard is contractual and the THD in the PCC should be less than 5%.
