Industrial emergency plants: sizing, transfer and maintenance

An emergency plant is only worth what it does the day the network fails. We specify, supply, integrate and maintain diesel and gas generators of any brand, with transfer, parallelism and testing that ensure they start and take charge.

Request diagnostic View maintenance program

Specification

Size by actual load, not transformer nameplate

An oversized group works with low load and accumulates engine problems; a subsized group does not start large engines. These are the criteria we reviewed before proposing capacity.

Operating regime

Emergency (standby), main (prime) or continuous, in accordance with ISO 8528-1. The same engine has different capacities in each regime.

Engine starting

Starting current, allowable voltage drop and load starting sequence so that the group does not stop when taking on a large motor.

Non-linear loads

Variables, UPS and rectifiers distort the current; they affect the choice of alternator and voltage regulator.

Site and autonomy

Altitude and temperature reduce available power. We define autonomy, tank, fuel supply, ventilation and noise.

Transfer

The transfer type decides what your process sees

The automatic transfer switch (ATS) is as critical as the engine. These are the options and what each one entails.

TypeHow it operatesWhat the load seesWhen is it appropriate
Open transitionDisconnect one source and then connect the otherA short break in each transfer, including return to the networkShear-tolerant loads; lowest cost option
Closed transitionBoth sources in parallel for a moment, already synchronizedNo interruption in network return and testingContinuous processes and periodic tests without affecting production
Soft transferSustained parallel; the load is transferred graduallyNo interruption or loading stepLarge or sensitive loads; requires interconnection protection
With bypass-isolationAllows the ATS to be removed for maintenance without de-energizingContinuity during maintenance of the ATS itselfFacilities that cannot schedule shutdowns

Any scheme with momentary or sustained parallel with the network requires synchronization, interconnection protections and review with CFE in accordance with the Network Code.

Various groups

Parallelism, redundancy and ballast shedding

Two or more groups in parallel provide N+1 redundancy and allow each machine to operate in its efficient range. In exchange, they require a controller that synchronizes them, distributes the load and disconnects loads when capacity is lacking.

Group Controllers

Specification, programming and commissioning of DEIF, Woodward and other manufacturers' controllers, integrated into the plant SCADA.

Board modernization

Replacement of obsolete control panels and synchrony with digital controllers, preserving the generator group when in good condition.

Hybrid with BESS

A BESS covers the start-up seconds, absorbs loading steps and reduces engine hours and fuel consumption in frequent-generation sites.

The complete logic of grid, generator, UPS, BESS and solar coordinated by an EMS is in Electrical Availability Architecture.

Maintenance and testing

The most common cause of a plant not starting is that no one tested it under load

Dead batteries, degraded fuel, and engines that have only run at idle fail just when they are needed. Our maintenance program adjusts to the criticality of the loads and the manufacturer's recommendations.

FrequencyActivities
Operational routineVisual inspection, levels, leaks, battery charger and condition, jacket heater, start-up and operation
PeriodicTest with real load or with load bank, verification of automatic transfer, protection and voltage regulator settings
AnnualExtended test with resistive load bank, oil and fuel analysis, thermography of panels and connections, alternator insulation tests
Corrective and modernizationDiagnosis of starting or charging socket faults, repair of alternators, retrofit of controllers and transfer boards

Reference regulations: current NOM-001-SEDE (emergency, legally required reserve and optional reserve systems), NFPA 110 for emergency and reserve power systems, and ISO 8528 for generating sets.

Frequently asked questions

Do they sell emergency plants of any particular brand?
We are not tied to one engine brand. We specify the group by the load and the site, we supply it through our multi-brand procurement and we assume the integration: transfer, control, protections, electrical work and testing.
Can you give maintenance to a plant you didn't install?
Yes. We started with a group diagnosis, transfer and switchboard, and a loaded test showing what condition the equipment is really in.
How long does it take a plant to take charge?
Depends on the group and the transfer; In emergency systems it is common to specify around 10 seconds between the loss of the network and the power supply to the loads. Loads that cannot tolerate that time require UPS or BESS.
Should the plant be replaced by a BESS?
For long autonomy, no. The combination is usually the best answer: the BESS covers the start-up and the peaks and the plant provides autonomy. We evaluate it against your load profile and site outage history.

Would your emergency plant start today with all the cargo?

We do a diagnosis with a load test and give you the real status of the group, the transfer and the control panel.
