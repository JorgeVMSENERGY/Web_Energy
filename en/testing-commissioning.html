Testing, commissioning, and startup: nothing is energized until it has been tested

The problem

The most expensive moment to find a fault is first energization

At the end of a project, the pressure is to start up. That is exactly when tests get skipped, factory settings get accepted, and equipment is handed over without evidence. These are the situations we see most:

Undetected shipping or installation damage

Equipment that passed its factory test can arrive with moisture, impact damage, or a loose connection. Without site testing, the damage shows up at energization.

Protection left at generic settings

Relays and breakers shipped with factory values instead of the settings from the coordination study: nuisance trips or, worse, protection that does not trip.

Several vendors, no one integrates

Switchgear from one vendor, the transformer from another, and the drive from a third. Each one tests its own equipment; no one tests the complete system working together.

Handover without evidence

Without signed protocols or reference values, warranty claims get harder and maintenance starts with no baseline to compare against.

How we do it

One sequence, two release points

Commissioning is not a test at the end: it is a sequence that starts before the equipment reaches the site. Nothing is energized without release from pre-operational testing, and nothing is handed over without accepted performance tests.

Equipment de-energized Equipment energized Release point: no progress without conforming results

Commissioning plan and protocols

We define what is tested, with what method, against which acceptance criteria, and who witnesses. It is agreed before construction, not during startup.

FAT witnessing

We attend factory acceptance tests for transformers, MCCs, sectionalizers, and drives to confirm the equipment meets its datasheet before it ships.

Site receipt and inspection

Check of shipping condition, nameplate data, visible damage, and installation and interconnection conditions before any electrical test.

Pre-operational electrical testing (SAT)

With the equipment de-energized: insulation, turns ratio, resistance, control wiring verification, protection settings and trip tests, interlocks.

Release for energization

Controlled energization and startup

Staged energization with scheduled switching, voltage and phase-sequence checks, and no-load behavior; configuration and startup of drives, generation, and control.

Functional and performance testing

Operation under real load: transfers, synchronization, control loops and communication with SCADA or DCS, and punch-list closeout with the client.

Client acceptance

The seventh stage, handover to operations, is described under deliverables. For technical detail on each transformer test, see our guides to FAT and SAT testingES and transformer installation and commissioningES.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between testing, commissioning, and startup?
Testing verifies the condition of each piece of equipment, usually de-energized. Commissioning is the complete sequence that confirms the system was installed and operates as designed, from the test plan through performance testing. Startup (putting into service) is the energization and handover to operations at the end of that sequence.
Can you commission equipment you did not supply or install?
Yes. We work multi-brand and have put into service equipment from different manufacturers supplied by third parties. In those cases we start with site receipt and inspection, and define the test protocol with the client and the manufacturer’s information.
Do you witness factory acceptance tests (FAT)?
Yes. We have taken part in FATs for transformers, motor control centers, 34.5 kV sectionalizers, and variable frequency drives before shipment, either as part of our supply or on behalf of the client.
What happens if a test does not meet the acceptance criteria?
The equipment is not released for energization. The result is documented, the cause is investigated with the manufacturer or installer, corrected, and the test is repeated. That is the purpose of the release points: a failure should never be discovered at energization.
What do you deliver at the end of commissioning?
A dossier with the plan and protocols, the records of every test, the final settings applied to protection, drives, and control, the closed punch list, and the handover to operations and maintenance staff.
Do you work offshore and outside Mexico?
Much of our testing and startup experience is on offshore platforms in the Campeche Sound, and we serve projects across Mexico (Tabasco, Veracruz, Quintana Roo, Durango, State of Mexico). For projects in the United States, Central America, or the Caribbean, tell us about the site and scope and we will confirm logistics and coverage.

Startup coming up?

Tell us which equipment you are putting into service and where your project stands. We will propose a testing and commissioning plan with scope, schedule, and deliverables.
