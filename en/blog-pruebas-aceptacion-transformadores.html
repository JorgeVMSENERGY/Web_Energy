FAT and SAT testing of industrial transformers: technical guide IEC 60076

Factory and on-site acceptance protocols: what is measured, how it is interpreted, and when to reject equipment.

A transformer can come out of perfect factory and get damaged to the site. Or it can meet all the parameters on paper and fail in service because no one verified the isolation after transportation. The acceptance tests —FAT in factory, SAT in field — are the last line of defense before energize a team that can cost hundreds of thousands of dollars and whose failure can stop a Full floor.

This guide documents the technical protocols that VMS Energy applies in EPC projects: what tests are performed, at what stage, how the results are interpreted and what are the rejection criteria according to the IEC 60076 series and IEEE C57 standards.

1. FAT vs SAT — differences and objectives

Both testing stages have different and complementary objectives. Confusing them—or substituting one for the other—is a mistake that can leave undetected vulnerabilities in the system.

Criterion FAT — Factory SAT — Site
When Before dispatch, with the finished equipment After assembly, before first power-up
Where Manufacturer facilities Final operating site
Objective Check compliance with order data sheet Confirm integrity after transportation and installation
Base regulations IEC 60076-1, IEC 60076-3, IEC 60076-2 IEC 60076-1, IEEE C57.12.91, project criteria
Who testifies Customer Engineer / EPC Supervisor EPC + Commissioner
If it fails The manufacturer repairs before shipping Equipment is not energized; cause is investigated
Fundamental principle: The FAT certifies what left the factory. The SAT certifies what will be installed in service. Neither replaces the other. In EPC projects, both protocols must be defined in the contract with the manufacturer from the specification phase.

2. Transformation Ratio (TTR)

FAT + SAT

The Turns Ratio Test (TTR) verifies that the relationship between the primary and secondary voltages of each tap changer matches what is declared on the data plate. It is the most fundamental test—if the transformation ratio is not correct, the transformer will not work for the application for which it was specified.

How to do it

A TTR (transformation ratio bridge) device is used that injects a low magnitude AC voltage to the primary and measures the voltage induced in the secondary, automatically calculating the ratio. The test is performed in all positions of the tap changer: nominal position, upper and lower limit, and at least one intermediate position.

Measured vs declared transformation ratio:

a = V1 /V2

Deviation (%) = |ameasure − anominal, / anominal × 100

IEC 60076-1 tolerance: ≤ ± 0.5 % in nominal position

Interpretation of results

  • Deviation < 0.5%: Compliant result. Register in protocol.
  • Deviation 0.5%–1.0%: Alert zone. Repeat test, check TTR equipment connections. If it persists, investigate with the manufacturer.
  • Deviation > 1.0%: Cause for rejection. It may indicate shorted turns, incorrect connection of the tap changer or error on the data plate.
  • Imbalance between phases in the same tap: Deviation greater than 0.3% between phases suggests an internal problem in one of the core columns.

3. Winding resistance

FAT + SAT

The winding resistance measurement (DC Winding Resistance) detects problems in the internal electrical circuit of the transformer: loose connections, poor soldering, short-circuited turns or problems in the tap changer contacts.

Procedure

A stabilized direct current (typically 10–25 A) is injected into each winding and the resulting voltage drop is measured with a high-precision micro-ohmmeter (minimum resolution of 1 µΩ). The winding must be completely demagnetized before the test; otherwise, the variations of the cola will induce significant errors. The measurement is repeated in each tap of the changer.

All values are corrected at the reference temperature of 75°C according to the formula of IEC 60076-1:

R75 = RT × (235 + 75) / (235 + T)

Where: RT = resistencia medida a temperatura T (°C)

235 = constant for copper (use 225 for aluminum)

Winding temperature should be measured at the time of testing and recorded in the protocol

Acceptance criteria

  • Imbalance between phases: maximum 2% for trifa devanates (IEC 60076-1)
  • Comparison with factory values (in SAT): the variation should not exceed 5% with respect to the corrected FAT protocol at the same temperature
  • Difference between taps: values should vary monotonically according to the changer; irregular jumps indicate poor contacts
Safety warning: HV windings store significant inductive energy. At the end of the test, never disconnect the cables before deactivating and downloading the measuring equipment. The sudden discharge can generate an arc and damage the insulation or cause accidents to personnel.

4. Short circuit impedance (Zcc) and load losses

Just a little bit of a fat.

The short circuit impedance test (or short circuit voltage, Ucc) measures the internal impedance of the transformer expressed as a percentage of the rated voltage. This value determines the maximum short circuit current that can circulate in the secondary, which is critical for the sizing of protective equipment.

Procedure

With the secondary short-circuited, the primary voltage increases until the nominal current circulates in both windings. The voltage at that point, expressed as a percentage of the nominal voltage, is the Ucc short circuit voltage (%). The power consumed in that state is the Loss on load or loss in copper (PCC), which includes ohmic losses in the windings and additional losses due to eddy currents.

Zcc (%) = UCC /UN × 100

IDC(HV) = SN / (√3 × UN(HV)) / Zcc

IEC 60076-1 tolerance: ±7.5% of the declared plate value for distribution transformers

Practical implications of Zcc value

Zcc (%) Short circuit current Voltage regulation Typical application
3.5 – 4.0 High (25–29× IN) Excellent (<3%) High breaking capacity, sensitive variable loads
4.5 – 5.75 Average (17–22× IN) Good (3–5%) Standard industrial distribution, most EPC projects
6.0 – 7.5 Low (13–17× IN) Moderate (5–7%) High power, lower breaking capacity switches, powering heavy loads

5. No-load losses and excitation current

Just a little bit of a fat.

The vacuum test (no-load test) is carried out with the secondary open, applying the nominal voltage to the primary. It measures two parameters that characterize the magnetic core of the transformer:

No-load losses (P’0)

Iron losses

Power consumed by hysteresis and eddy currents in the core. They are present provided the transformer is energized, regardless of the load. Its value should not exceed 5% of the guaranteed value.

No-load current (I’0 %)

Excitation current

Percentage of the nominal current necessary to magnetize the core. Typically 0.2–2% in modern transformers. High values ​​may indicate damaged core sheets or incorrect stacking pressure.

Importance in the life cycle

No-load losses represent the permanent energy cost of the transformer. In projects with Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) evaluation, a transformer with 20% higher no-load losses can cost several thousand additional dollars in energy over its useful life. That is why demanding EPC specifications establish guaranteed maximum values ​​of P'0, not just tolerances with respect to the declared value.

6. Insulation power factor (tan delta)

FAT + SAT

The insulation power factor—known as tangent delta (tan δ) or dissipation factor (DF)—is the most sensitive measure of the condition of the transformer insulation system. It quantifies the dielectric losses of solid (paper) and liquid (oil or resin) insulation expressed as a quotient between the resistive current (real losses) and the total capacitive current.

tan δ = IR / IC = P / (U × ITotal)

An ideal isolation would be purely capacitive: so

In new transformers: tan δ < 0.005 (0.5%) at 20°C — IEC 60250

Measurement Settings

The test is performed with the transformer de-energized, using a Schering bridge or equivalent equipment. Three classic configurations are measured:

  • CH (HT winding to ground, LV winding shorted to ground): Measures the insulation of the high voltage winding towards ground and towards the LV winding.
  • CL (LV winding, HV shorted to ground): Measures the insulation of the low voltage winding.
  • CHL (HV to LV, both floating): Measures the insulation between windings, particularly sensitive to the condition of the paper between layers.
so Interpretation Recommended action
< 0.5 Excellent — sound insulation Register as base. Energize.
0.5 – 1.0 Good — acceptable on new equipment Register. Compare with FAT values.
1.0 – 2.0 Precautionary area Investigate if the FAT value was lower. Review storage and transportation conditions. Consider drying.
> 2.0 (new equipment) Unacceptable Do not energize. Report to the manufacturer. Possible insulation damage or contamination.
> 3× valor FAT (SAT) Transport damage or moisture Do not energize. Drying process or return to factory.

7. High voltage dielectric tests

Only FAT (routine)

Dielectric tests verify that the transformer insulation can withstand the surges to which it will be subjected in service. IEC 60076-3 establishes two routine tests and additional type tests for high voltage levels.

Applied voltage test (ACWV)

Applied Voltage Withstand

A separate AC voltage is applied to each winding during 60 seconds. The test voltage is a function of the winding insulation level (NBI). Test the insulation between windings and between each winding and ground.

Induced voltage (A/C/IVPD)

Induced Voltage

A voltage is induced between terminals of the same winding at a high frequency (100–400 Hz) to avoid saturation. Tests the insulation between turns, between layers and between windings under operating conditions.

Type tests — lightning impulse (LI) and switching impulse (SI)

For transformers with rated voltage ≥ 72.5 kV, lightning impulse test (LI) is mandatory as a type test. A 1.2/50 µs pulse is applied with an amplitude defined by the Basic Impulse Level (NBI) of the device. The wave must be recorded on a high-speed digital oscilloscope and compared with the reference wave. Any wave deformation is evidence of internal failure.

EPC Note: Type tests (LI, SI, heating) are usually performed only once per manufacturer design, not on each unit. In critical projects, the customer may require proof of LI per unit; This must be negotiated in the technical and commercial specification before ordering, as it involves additional cost and time.

8. Dissolved Gas Analysis (DGA)

Priority in SAT

Dissolved Gas Analysis is the most informative diagnostic tool available for oil transformers. The different types of electrical or thermal failure generate characteristic gases that remain dissolved in the oil and can be identified by gas chromatography (GC).

Key gases and their meaning

Gas Symbol Probable origin Alert level (new equipment)
Hydrogen H₂ Partial oil discharge, slight overheating > 100ppm
Methane CH₄ Oil overheating (<300°C) > 120ppm
Ethylene C₂H₄ Severe oil overheating (>500°C) > 50 ppm
Ethane C₂H₆ Moderate oil overheating > 65 ppm
Acetylene C₂H₂ Electric arc in oil > 1 ppm (any level is alarm)
Carbon monoxide CO Thermal degradation of insulating paper (<300°C) > 350 ppm
Carbon dioxide CO₂ Paper degradation (>300°C) or normal aging > 2,500 ppm

Method of interpretation: Rogers relationship and IEC 60599 codes

Gases are not interpreted in isolation but through relationships between them. The method of IEC 60599 codes (revised from Rogers) classifies probable failure by calculating three ratios: CH₄/H₂, C₂H₂/C₂H₄ and C₂H₄/C₂H₆. Duval's method offers a graphic triangle that allows identifying the type of failure with greater robustness when any of the quotients are undefined by zero values.

SAT protocol for oil transformers: Take sample before first energization to confirm that the filling was correct and set baseline. Repeat 24 hours after first energy and compare. An explosive increase of any gas between both samples is a sign of active failure.

10. Case study: 2,500 kVA transformer SAT, Dyn11

SAT Case — Processing plant, Sonora

DEEMSA 2,500 kVA transformer, 34.5 kV / 480 V, Dyn11, ONAN, Zcc 5.75%

Context: EPC project for mineral processing plant in Sonora, 1,500 meters above sea level. Transformer transported by flatbed truck from Monterrey (560 km). The unit arrived without apparent incident according to the impact accelerometer, but the full SAT protocol was executed per project specification.

SAT results:

  • Physical inspection: No visible mechanical damage. Nitrogen pressure (sealed preservative): 2.1 psi positive — correct, indicates intact seal.
  • TTR (transformation relationship): Phases A: 71.87 (nominal: 71.88), B: 71.89, C: 71.87. Max deviation 0.013% — COMPLIANT
  • Winding resistance (HV at 23°C): A: 14.82 Ω, B: 14.85 Ω, C: 14.83 Ω. Imbalance: 0.20% — COMPLIANT
  • Delta tangent (CH): 0.38% — Registered FAT: 0.31%. Variation: +22.6%, within 3× tolerance. COMPLIANT
  • Dielectric strength of oil (before energizing): 52 kV/mm — minimum limit 30 kV/mm (IEC 60156) — COMPLIANT
  • Moisture in oil: 8.2 ppm — 35 ppm limit for 34.5 kV (IEC 60422) — COMPLIANT
  • DGA before energizing: All gases < 5 ppm. acetylene: not detected — COMPLIANT
  • DGA at 24 hours of operation: H₂: 12 ppm, CH₄: 8 ppm, all other gases < 5 ppm, acetylene: not detected — NORMAL

Conclusion: Transformer approved for commercial operation. Baseline DGA is documented as a reference for the 6 month predictive maintenance program.

Frequently asked questions about FAT and SAT tests

What's the difference between FAT and SAT in transformers?

The FAT (Factory Acceptance Test) is performed at the manufacturer's facilities, before the equipment is shipped. Verify that the transformer was built according to the order data sheet. The SAT (Site Acceptance Test) is executed at the installation site, after transportation and assembly, confirming that the equipment arrived without damage and that the installation is correct before the first commercial energization. Some parameters are measured in both stages; others are exclusive to each one.

What tolerance does IEC 60076 accept in the transformation ratio?

In accordance with IEC 60076-1, the maximum acceptable deviation in the transformation ratio (TTR) is ±0.5% from the declared value, when measured at the nominal bypass. Larger deviations indicate short circuit between turns or incorrect internal connection and are cause for rejection.

When is the DGA done and what gases are the most critical?

The DGA applies only to oil-filled transformers. In FAT, it can optionally be taken as a basal value. In SAT, it is mandatory before the first energization and is repeated 24–72 hours later. The most critical gas is acetylene (C2H2): any concentration—even 1 ppm—is an unequivocal sign of an electric arc and immediate cause for investigation. Hydrogen (>100 ppm), ethylene (>50 ppm) and CO (>350 ppm) are next in priority.

What is tangent delta and when does it worry?

Tangent delta (tan δ) measures the dielectric losses of the insulation. A perfect isolation would have tan δ = 0; humidity, pollution and aging raise it. In new transformers, the value must be less than 0.5% (0.005) at 20°C. A value greater than 2.0% in new equipment is cause for rejection. In SAT, a value greater than three times that recorded in the FAT indicates possible moisture ingress during transportation or storage and requires investigation before energizing.

How is the resistance of devanados measured and what does it detect?

A micro-ohmmeter is used that injects stabilized DC current and measures the voltage drop. The values ​​are corrected to 75°C (IEC 60076-1). The test detects loose connections, poor soldering, shorted turns and faulty contacts in the tap changer. The imbalance between phases must not exceed 2%. In SAT, the variation with respect to the FAT protocol should not be greater than 5%.

Does the result of the FAT guarantee that the transformer will get to the site well?

No. The FAT guarantees that it left the factory according to specification. Transportation may cause shock, vibration, humidity due to failure of the positive pressure seal, or incorrect oil filling in the field. That is why the SAT is mandatory and independent of the FAT. At a minimum, the SAT should include TTR, winding resistance, tan delta, and—in oil-filled transformers—dielectric oil analysis (stiffness, moisture, DGA) prior to first energization.

What dielectric tests are mandatory in FAT?

According to IEC 60076-3, the mandatory routine dielectric tests in FAT are: applied voltage (ACWV), which subjects each winding to a separate AC voltage for 60 seconds; and proof of induced voltage (A/PDIV/PDV), which induces high voltage between terminals of the same winding at high frequency to test the insulation between turns. For HV transformers (≥ 72.5 kV), lightning impulse (LI) and switching impulse (SI) tests are added as type tests.
