Challenges in the sector

The electrical pressure that every director of a sugar mill faces

The sugar industry operates under conditions that have no equivalent in other sectors: rigid seasonality, short maintenance windows and a cost of unemployment during the harvest that can exceed $500,000 MXN per hour.

01

Electrical seasonality without margin of error

During the harvest (November–May), the power plant operates at 100% capacity, 24 hours a day, for six continuous months. A failure in the turbogenerator, substation or Woodward governor can cost between $50,000 and $500,000 MXN per hour of lost production. There is no second chance: the cut cane deteriorates in hours and the grinding schedule cannot be recovered.

02

Massive starts in pre-harvest

At the beginning of the season, the mill starts dozens of high-torque motors—mills, diffusers, steam pumps, evaporators—in a few hours. Poor coordination of protections, a poorly configured drive or a faulty synchronization switchgear can generate chain trips that stop the entire factory right on start-up day. The pre-harvest is the time of greatest electrical risk of the year.

03

Short window for projects and investment

Repair (May–October) is the only window to execute important electrical projects: substation rehabilitation, updating of governors, installation of new CCMs or cables. If imported materials are delayed or engineering is not ready, the harvest starts with substandard equipment — and the next repair cycle is six months away.

Our solutions

Electrical services for sugar mills

The sugar industry has critical electrical requirements: own generation with turbosteam, interconnection substations with CFE and intensive maintenance during repair. VMS Energy covers the entire spectrum.

Steam Turbogenerators — Woodward

Programming and commissioning of Woodward 505, 505-XT and 2301D governors for counterpressure and extraction-condensation turbines. PID loop adjustment, overspeed test and synchronization with CFE network. Experience in more than 12 mills throughout Mexico.

MV/HV power substations

Design, construction and rehabilitation of 13.8–69 kV substations for sugar mills. Acceptance tests (TTR, VLF, contact resistance), synchronization switchgear for parallelism with CFE and major maintenance of transformers and switches.

Power plant automation

SCADA integration of the mill's power plant: monitoring of generation, distribution and consumption. Automation of process equipment: mills, diffusers, evaporators and centrifuges. Updating obsolete boards to modern PLCs with industrial Ethernet communication.

Predictive maintenance in repair

Electrical Thermography of Boards, MCC and Substation. Transformer Diagnostics (TTR, megger, DGA) during the annual repair stop. VLF test on MV cables to evaluate insulation status before the next harvest.

MCC and force distribution

Supply and installation of IEC 61439 Motor Control Centers (MCC) for factory, batey and cogeneration areas. Updating of mechanical starters to frequency variable frequency drives for energy saving in pumps and fans.

Power factor correction

Study of measurement, design and installation of APFC banks to correct the power factor before CFE and avoid penalties. Mills with high motor and furnace loads can reach FPs of 0.75–0.82, generating significant additional charges.

Seasonality of the sector

Electric cycle of the sugar mill

The sugar industry operates in defined annual cycles. VMS Energy accompanies the mill at each stage with the appropriate electrical services to maximize availability during the harvest.

Repair (May–Oct)

Major maintenance of turbines, governors and substation. Thermography, VLF, transformer diagnosis. Updating boards and MCC. Ideal time for EPC projects.

Pre-harvest (Oct–Nov)

Commissioning of Woodward turbogenerators and governors. Switch and switchgear testing. Adjustment of electrical protections. SCADA verification and control systems.

Zafra (Nov-May)

Emergency technical support, 2007. Response in less than 24 hours to failures in governors or electrical systems. Unscheduled stoppage costs between 50,000 and 500,000 BDN per hour.

Closing of the harvest (Apr–May)

Analysis of electrical performance of the harvest. Identification of improvements for the next season. Investment planning and repair projects.
