Scope of service

From route engineering to protocol delivery

Industrial electrical wiring does not end when the cable arrives at the site. It ends when the test protocol is signed and the system operates within the design parameters.

Route and pipeline engineering

Design of optimal trajectories, selection of the type of channeling (ladder tray, channel, ventilated, EMT/IMC/rigid conduit), segregation between power, control and instrumentation circuits, load calculation per section and supports. Delivery of floor plans and isometrics.

Manufacture and installation of trays and conduit

Custom manufacturing, cutting, bending and connection of metal trays and conduit. Installation of supports with spacing calculation in accordance with NEC and NOM-001-SEDE. Anticorrosive treatment in aggressive environments (offshore, petrochemical, food). Ground continuity throughout the network.

Cable twine, LV, MV and HV

Tended with minimum curvature radius by voltage level (IEC 60502), maximum pull voltage in large conductors (kcmil), ambient installation temperature and separation between circuits. Use of lubricant compatible with cable cover. Recording of tracked Kayas.

Certified terminations and splices

Thermo-retractile and resin finishes for LV (3M Scotchcast, TE Connect 98). Internal/outdoor dry-end kits and MV/HV joints (3M QT-III, Raychem HVT, Nexans). Installation by certified personnel; each completed completion documented with the kit used, serial number and installation responsible.

Electrical tests with protocol

Insulation resistance (megger 500V to 10kV depending on voltage level), conductor continuity, screen resistance, Hi-Pot DC or VLF dielectric strength test (0.1 Hz). Each test delivered in protocol with measured values, normative comparison and signature of the technical manager.

Firestop sealing of ducts

Installation of fire sealants in cable passages through partitions, slabs and walls classified in accordance with NOM-002-STPS and insurer requirements. 3M Firestop, Hilti CP and Rockwool systems. Installation certificate delivered by sealing point to support audits.

EPC Methodology

How we execute a wiring project

Each project follows the same control sequence — without skipping steps — regardless of the size of the contract.

1

Route engineering

We review the electrical single-line, visit the site, define trajectories, select the channeling system and calculate the tray loadability. We issue plans for the client's review before starting work.

2

Manufacture and installation of pipeline

We manufacture and install trays, conduit and supports according to approved plans. We verify ground continuity throughout the conduit system before laying the cable.

3

Cable tending

Tended with ambient temperature, curvature radius and maximum pull voltage. Recording of the personal personal data by circuit. Provisional labelling at both ends for identification before completion.

4

Terminations and splices

Preparation and execution of terminations by certified personnel. Record of the kit used, manufacturer's batch and installation person. Visual inspection of each termination prior to electrical testing.

5

Electrical tests

Megger, continuity, Hi-Pot or VLF depending on the voltage level. Each test documented in an individual protocol with the equipment used (with a current calibration certificate), measured values, environmental conditions and signature of the responsible technician.

6

Delivery and As-Built

Update of plans to As-Built condition, compilation of the installation dossier (test protocols, completion records, materials certificates) and presentation of results to the client. The system is ready for commissioning.

Electrical tests

Without signed protocol, the cable is not delivered

Each installed circuit is tested before delivery. The measured values ​​are compared against the manufacturer's and regulatory limits. The tests are the client's technical support against any future failure.

Megger IEC 60502

Insulation resistance

Measurement with digital tellurometer at 500 V (LV), 1–5 kV (MV) or 10 kV (HV). Detects moisture, contamination or mechanical damage to the insulation. It is performed before and after terminations to separate the state of the cable from the state of the termination.

Hi-Pot DC · IEEE 400.2

DC dielectric strength

Application of DC voltage at 0.6 × Un × √2 for 15 minutes with recording of the leakage current. A stable or decreasing current indicates healthy insulation. It applies to new or rehabilitated MV and HV cables. Does not damage the XLPE unlike the Hi-Pot AC power test.

VLF · IEEE 400.2

Very Low Frequency Test (0.1 Hz)

Test AC at 0.1 Hz as an alternative to Hi-Pot DC on HV cables. Prevents space charge buildup in XLPE that can cause the Hi-Pot DC "memory" effect. It is complemented by measurement of dielectric losses (tan δ) to diagnose the state of the insulation.

PD IEC 60885-3

Partial discharge detection

Measurement of partial discharges (partial discharge) in HV cables and MV/HV terminations. Detects manufacturing defects, mechanical damage to the display, or incorrect installation of terminations before they cause catastrophic failure. Recommended in new HV cables and in rehabilitations.

Micro-ohm · IEC 60228

Driver and screen resistance

DC resistance measurement of the conductor in micro-ohms to verify the actual cross section and the absence of hidden splices or damage to the conductor. The continuity of the metal shield is checked to ensure the return of ground fault currents.

IR · NET ATS

Post-service termography

Infrared thermographic inspection of terminations and connectors in the first 30 days of operation under load. A hot spot on a termination indicates insufficient tightening or a defect in the kit before it causes failure. We deliver the thermographic report as part of the installation dossier.
