Technical scope

10 parameters with standard and acceptance criteria

Each analysis includes comparison against IEC 60422 limit values, with RAG condition classification (green · yellow · red) by parameter and by equipment.

IEC 60156 ASTM D877/D1816

Dielectric strength (BDV)

Cup test with standardized electrode, 5–6 averaged measurements. Detects humidity and solid particles in suspension.

MV ≥ 30 kV/mm | HV ≥ 50 kV/mm
New oil: ≥ 60 kV/mm
< 20 kV/mm → emergency

IEC 60814 Karl Fischer

Moisture content (H₂O)

Karl Fischer coulometric titulation. Humidity reduces dielectric rigidity and accelerates the aging of insulating paper.

MV < 25 ppm | HV < 10 ppm
New oil < 5 ppm
> 35 ppm → immediate dehydration

ASTM D974 · IEC 62021-1

Acidity (neutralization index)

Measures organic acids produced by oil oxidation. Corrosive acids degrade paper and internal metals.

Normal < 0.15 mgKOH/g
Caution 0.15–0.30 | Action > 0.30
> 0.50 mgKOH/g → reemplazo

ASTM D971 · IEC 60422

Interfacial voltage

Measurement of voltage at the oil-water interface. It decreases with the presence of oxidation products and sludge.

Normal > 28 mN/m
Caution 22–28 | Action < 22 mN/m
< 18 mN/m → regeneration

IEC 60247 ASTM D924

Dielectric power factor (tan δ)

Measured at 90 °C. Indicates dielectric losses; rises with pollution, aging and presence of water.

Normal < 0.05% (90 °C)
Caution 0.05–0.20%
> 0.50% → corrective action

ASTM D1524

ASTM Color

Visual indicator of oil aging. Progressive darkening signals oxidation and sludge formation.

New oil ≤ 0.5
In service: ≤ 3.5 acceptable
> 5.0 → very degraded oil

ASTM D445

Cinematic viscosity

Measurement at 40 °C. It affects oil circulation and cooling efficiency. Rises by thermal polymerization.

Mineral oil class II: ≤ 11 mm²/s (40 °C)
Manufacturer reference as baseline value

ASTM D92 ASTM D93

Flash point

Minimum temperature at which the vapors are inflamed. A significant decrease suggests contamination with solvents or fuels.

New oil ≥ 135 °C
In service ≥ 125 °C
Descenso > 15 °C → investigar

ASTM D2668 · IEC 60666

Oxidation inhibitor (DBPC)

Concentration of 2,6-ditertbutylparacresol. Inhibited oils lose protection when DBPC is depleted and oxidation accelerates.

Inhibited oil: 0.20–0.35% m/m
< 0.08% → reponer inhibidor

IEC 60450 · ASTM D4243

Degree of paper polymerization (DP)

Insulating paper sampling. DP indicates residual useful life of solid insulation — irreversible once degraded.

New DP 1000–1200
DP < 400 → 50% vida consumida
DP < 200 → risk of rupture

Advanced diagnosis

DGA: Dissolved Gas Analysis by Chromatography

DGA is the most powerful predictive technique for oil transformers. Detects incipient failures months in advance by quantifying 7 key gases.

Duval Triangle

IEC 60599

It uses the relative percentages of CH4, C2H4 and C2H2 to classify the type of failure: partial discharges (PD), overheating T1/T2/T3 and D1/D2 arcs. Graphically included in each report.

Rogers method

IEEE C57.104

Gas ratios (CH₄/H₂, C₂H₄/C₂H₆, C₂H₂/C₂H₄) to classify failure by temperature and energy. It complements Duval's triangle in cases in border areas.

Trend Analysis

IEC 60422

The gas generation rate (ppm/month) is as important as the absolute value. An increasing trend in acetylene or hydrogen, although below the threshold, may indicate active failure.

The 7 gases of DGA: origin and diagnostic meaning

Gas Formula Main origin Threshold IEEE C57.104 (Cond. 2) Indicator
Hydrogen H₂ Partial discharges, slight overheating 100ppm DP/overheat
Methane CH₄ Oil overheating (< 300 °C) 120ppm Mild thermal
Ethane C₂H₆ Oil overheating (150–300 °C) 65ppm Moderate thermal
Ethylene C₂H₄ Severe overheating oil (> 300 °C) 50ppm Severe thermal
Acetylene C₂H₂ Electric arcs (> 700 °C) 35ppm Electric arc
Carbon monoxide CO Degradation of insulating paper (cellulose) 350ppm Gradient paper
Carbon dioxide CO₂ Normal/accelerated paper degradation 2500ppm Paper reference

* TDCG (Total Dissolved Fuel Gas) = ​​H₂ + CH₄ + C₂H₂ + C₂H₄ + C₂H₆ + CO. Condition 1: < 720 ppm (normal). Condition 2: 720–1920 ppm (caution). Condition 3: 1920–4630 ppm (action). Condition 4: > 4630 ppm (emergency). Source: IEEE C57.104.

Service flow

From the sample to the action plan in 6 stages

A structured process that guarantees sample traceability, analytical rigour and actionable recommendations for the customer maintenance team.

01

Fleet survey

Transformer inventory, kVA, voltage level, oil type, previous analysis history and load conditions. Defines sampling priority.

02

Sampling according to IEC 60475

Sample under load (hot) with hermetic glass syringe. No bubbles, no air pollution. Documented chain of custody to the laboratory.

03

Laboratory analysis

GC-FID/TCD chromatograph for the 7 gases. Cup test IEC 60156 (BDV rigidity). Karl Fischer (humidity). Coulometry and spectrophotometry for the rest of the parameters.

04

Expert diagnosis

Duval + Rogers interpretation + historical trend analysis. RAG classification by parameter. Comparison with values from the previous campaign.

05

Technical report

PDF report by equipment with graphed Duval triangle, IEC 60422 comparative table, RAG condition and prioritized recommendations (immediate / 6 months / annual).

06

Monitoring program

Calendar of the next sampling campaign, individual threshold alerts by equipment and historical database in Excel for integration to the client's CMMS.

Frequently asked questions

What maintenance teams ask us

How often should a transformer's oil be analyzed?
According to IEC 60422: new transformers in the first 2 years — once a year. Equipment in continuous service without anomalies — every 12 to 24 months. With a history of problems, high load or aggressive environment — every 6 months. When the DGA shows an upward trend in any key gas — monthly frequency until stabilizing. VMS Energy recommends setting the first test within 90 days of operation as a baseline.
What does the presence of acetylene (C2H2) indicate in the DGA?
Acetylene is generated exclusively at temperatures above 700 °C, directly associated with internal electric arcs. Any detection of acetylene — even 1 to 2 ppm — is a red flag requiring immediate trend analysis. Concentrations greater than 35 ppm (Condition 3, IEEE C57.104) indicate possible active failure and the need to remove the equipment for internal inspection before re-energizing.
When is oil/regenerate replaced?
Filtering (dehydration and degassing) recovers dielectric strength and reduces humidity, but does not eliminate the oxidation products of the aged oil. If the acidity exceeds 0.5 mgKOH/g, the ASTM color exceeds 4.0 or the tan δ at 90 °C exceeds 1%, the oil must be regenerated or replaced. Total replacement is the only option when the insulating paper is degraded (DP < 200), since regenerating the oil in that scenario does not solve the underlying problem.
Can the oil sample be taken with the energized transformer?
Yes. Hot sampling — with the energized and low load equipment — is the preferred protocol because it represents the actual operating conditions and ensures that the dissolved gases have not been dispersed. It is done through the lower sampling valve with bubble-free water-glass syringe, according to IEC 60475. The VMS Energy technician uses personal protective equipment for the voltage level and executes the sampling without interrupting the customer process.
What is the Duval Triangle and how is it interpreted?
Duval's Triangle (IEC 60599) uses the relative percentages of methane (CH₄), ethylene (C₂H₄) and acetylene (C₂H₂) to classify the type of failure: PD (partial discharge), T1/T2/T3 (mild/moderate/severe overheating at <300°C / 300–700°C / >700°C) and D1/D2 (low or high energy arc). The equipment coordinate in the triangle defines the predominant fault zone. VMS Energy includes the graphical triangle in all its reports, with the historical evolution of the coordinate when there are previous measurements.
What level of dielectric stiffness (BDV) is unacceptable to continue operating?
According to IEC 60422: MV transformers (up to 36 kV) — minimum acceptable 30 kV/mm; target value ≥ 40 kV/mm. HV transformers (72.5 kV and above) — acceptable minimum 50 kV/mm. For new oil before filling, VMS Energy requires ≥ 60 kV/mm. A result below 20 kV/mm at any voltage level is an emergency: the oil must be filtered or replaced before continuing operation.
Do dry transformers (class F or H) require physical-chemical oil analysis?
No. Dry transformers (cast resin or VPI) do not contain dielectric oil and therefore the FQ analysis or DGA does not apply. For dry transformers, predictive diagnosis is performed using infrared thermography, Megger and polarization index, resin dielectric power factor, and partial discharge (PD) analysis. VMS Energy applies the correct protocol depending on the type of insulation of the equipment.
Does VMS Energy also perform oil treatment or rehabilitation?
FQ and DGA analysis is the diagnostic service that VMS Energy runs directly. If the report concludes that the oil requires treatment, we coordinate with laboratories specialized in regeneration and filtration using mobile vacuum dehydration-degassing plants. We also manage the supply of new oil when the diagnosis indicates total replacement, ensuring that the oil meets IEC 60296 class II specifications before filling.
