The challenge: a damaged drive, without spare parts, in a critical compressor

The Colima bottling company distributes its drinks in the north of the state of Colima and the south of Jalisco. In an operation that size, every link in the production process must be kept moving.

The problem: the drive that controlled a compressor The production process was old and damaged, and it had to be replaced urgently. The brand installed no longer had any spare parts or immediate delivery equipment, so there was no way to repair it in time. Every hour without such a compressor was a direct risk to the continuity of production.

The challenge was not only technical, but also time-sensitive: replace damaged equipment with one available and reliable, without stopping operation.

Why a Speed changer makes the difference

A speed changer (or frequency changer) regulates how a compressor’s engine works by adjusting the frequency and voltage it receives. Instead of always operating at full load, the equipment diulates its performance according to the actual demand of the process. That reduces the mechanical and electrical stress at each start-up and makes energy consumption more efficient.

The key in this case was the PID control modes. A PID loop automatically adjusts the compressor speed to maintain a process variable—pressure, for example—at its target value, correcting deviations in real time. The result is equipment that responds only to production conditions, with greater stability and less manual intervention.

For a bottler, that translates into a more stable process, a better protected compressor, and an operation that is not dependent on an obsolete part.

The solution: field survey and a 200 HP drive

VMS Energy started with a field survey to verify the engine plate data, distances, permissives and general conditions of the installation. This diagnosis allowed selecting the correct speed changer for the compressor, instead of blindly replacing it.

From there, VMS Energy carried out the engineering, supply, installation and commissioning of a 200 HP Triol speed variable, designed to automatically control a wide range of production equipment and configured with PID control modes. The unit performed successfully in a 4-hour test and was fully operational.

What this means for your plant

The Colima case shows a silent risk that many food and beverage plants face: obsolescence. A drive that has been in operation for years can become the single point of failure the day it breaks and there are no longer any spare parts. When this happens in a critical compressor, the problem stops being maintenance and becomes a production risk.

Three practical readings:

  • The availability of spare parts is as important as specifications: a good unsupported equipment is a latent risk.
  • PID control converts a simple replacement into a process performance improvement.
  • One person responsible — from raising in the field to starting up — resolves urgency without fragmenting responsibility.

It is not just about changing a drive: it is about diagnosing well, choosing available and reliable equipment, and leaving it operating validated.

Frequently asked questions

What is a speed changer and what is it for in a compressor?

It is a device that regulates the speed of the compressor motor by adjusting the frequency and voltage it receives. It allows performance to be modulated according to process demand, reduce start-up stress and, with PID control modes, automatically maintain a variable such as pressure at its target value.

Why does the availability of spare parts matter so much when choosing a variator?

Because equipment without spare parts or immediate delivery becomes a risk when it fails. At the Colima bottling plant, the installed brand did not have spare parts available, which forced an urgent replacement. Choosing equipment with support and availability avoids prolonged downtime.

What did VMS Energy do in the Colima bottler?

Conducted a field survey to verify plate data, distances, permits and conditions; Based on that, it selected, designed, supplied, installed and commissioned a 200 HP Triol variable speed drive with PID control, which passed a 4-hour test and was fully operational.
