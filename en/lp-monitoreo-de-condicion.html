Why monitor

Failure rarely begins with unemployment

First the condition of the equipment changes: the vibration increases, a bearing heats up, the current becomes unbalanced. Monitoring detects that signal and maintenance takes action before major damage occurs. The value is in seeing the failure in time to plan the intervention, not in reacting to the strike.

From data to decision

Architecture of a condition monitoring system

We design, supply and integrate each layer. If your plant already has PLC, SCADA or DCS, we take advantage of it: monitoring adds to the existing infrastructure instead of creating an island of data.

Critical equipment

Motors, pumps, fans, compressors, reducers and turbines selected by criticality, not by inertia.

Sensors

Accelerometers, RTDs, current transformers, tachometers and process variables, wired or wireless.

Acquisition

PLC, gateway or edge equipment that digitizes, filters and sends information with industrial protocols.

SCADA / Historian

Operational and historical context: vibration is interpreted together with load, speed and process.

Analysis

Trends, FFT spectrum and severity alarms, with base line specific to each team.

Predictive maintenance

Work order with probable cause and priority. It intervenes before the stoppage and is verified afterwards.

What we monitor

Variables that reveal the real condition of the equipment

No variable tells the whole story. The correct combination depends on the type of equipment and its most likely failure modes.

ISO 20816 / ISO 13373

Vibration

RMS speed for global severity; acceleration and envelope for bearings and gears. Detecta desEygo, misalignment, mechanical slack, bearing wear and resonance.

Pt100 RTD/thermocouple

Temperature

Bearings, pillow blocks, windings and casing. A sustained increase confirms what the vibration anticipates: poor lubrication, overload or cooling problems.

Measurement in MCC / drive

Current and electrical variables

Consumption, imbalance between phases and load trend. Reveals engine and driven equipment problems that are not always seen in vibration.

Tacometer / phase reference

Speed (RPM)

Essential in equipment with a drive: it allows you to compare readings at different speeds and identify which frequencies of the spectrum correspond to each fault.

Pressure · flow · level

Process

The operating context that avoids false alarms: a pump outside its operating point vibrates differently than one with damaged bearings.

API 670 (critical machinery)

Machinery protection

In critical turbines and compressors, monitoring also protects: shaft displacement, axial position and automatic tripping against dangerous vibration.

Severity criteria

Reference vibration levels (ISO 20816-3)

RMS speed in mm/s, measured in the housing of industrial machines greater than 15 kW. They are the starting point: the end alarm thresholds are adjusted with the baseline of each equipment and the manufacturer's recommendation.

Service modalities

Start where your plant is today

Not all equipment needs online monitoring. Criticality, redundancy, cost of downtime and access to equipment define the correct technique for each.

Starting point

Criticality diagnosis

Inventory of rotating assets, failure history and criticality matrix. Result: which equipment to monitor, with what variables and with what frequency, and a scope estimate.

Medium criticality equipment

Route monitoring

Periodic readings with portable equipment, with trends and condition report per equipment. Executed with specialist allies and coordinated by VMS.

Critical or difficult to access equipment

Online monitoring

Permanent sensors, gateway and link to the plant SCADA or DCS, with severity alarms. Ideal for teams without redundancy, remote or offshore.

Closing of the cycle

O&M and response

Maintenance of motors and rotating equipment, replacement of bearings, alignment, adjustment of protections and post-intervention verification.

Frequently asked questions

Questions about condition monitoring

How is condition monitoring different from preventive maintenance?

The preventive intervenes by calendar or hours of operation, whether or not the equipment is damaged. Condition monitoring measures the actual condition of the equipment and schedules intervention when data warrants it. Reduces both unexpected downtime and unnecessary maintenance.

Do I need online monitoring or are regular routes enough?

Depends on criticality. Equipment without backup, with costly downtime or difficult to access (offshore, remote) justify online monitoring. Equipment with redundancy or less impact is usually well covered with monthly or quarterly routes. The criticality diagnosis defines the appropriate mixture.

Can you integrate into the SCADA or DCS I already have?

Yes. Current sensors and #Turns communicate through industrial protocols such as Modbus TCP/RTU, Profinet, EtherNet/IP or OPC UA. We integrate signals and alarms into your existing system so that operation sees the condition of the equipment along with the process.

What failures does vibration analysis detect?

Imbalance, misalignment, mechanical looseness, wear of bearings and gears, cavitation in pumps and structural resonances, among others. Combined with temperature and current, it also helps distinguish electrical problems with the motor from mechanical problems with the driven equipment.

What standard defines acceptable vibration levels?

The reference for industrial machines is the ISO 20816 series (formerly ISO 10816), which classifies severity into zones A, B, C and D according to machine size and type of base. For critical process machinery, API 670 defines protection systems. Thresholds are adjusted with each team's baseline.

Does it work on frequency-variator equipment?

Yes, provided that speed is recorded along with vibration, this compares readings at different speeds and correctly identifies the frequencies of failure in the spectrum.

Where should we start?

For a criticality diagnosis and a driver in the teams that cost the most when they stop. With measurable results in those teams, it is decided whether to expand the scope.

What equipment from your plant can't fail?

Let's start with a criticality diagnosis: we identify the equipment that costs the most when it stops and we define what to monitor, how and how often.
