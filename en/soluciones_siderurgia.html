Why a steel mill's electrical network is different

Few industrial loads punish the grid as much as a melting furnace. Added to this are high-power motors that start and stop continuously, and auxiliary services that cannot fail without putting the equipment at risk. These are the problems that cost the most in the plant:

Flicker and harmonics

The arc furnace demands reactive power erratically and the induction furnace injects harmonics. The result: voltage fluctuations, overheated transformers, protection trips, penalties and Grid Code compliance observations.

Demand peaks

Casts and large engine starts raise the maximum demand measured in the GDMTH rate. A single poorly managed peak is reflected in the capacity charge for the entire month.

Harsh environment for electrical equipment

Radiant heat, conductive metal dust and vibration accelerate the deterioration of boards, transformers, connections and motors. Failures appear earlier than the maintenance schedule indicates.

Unbacked critical services

If you lose cooling water from furnaces or hydraulics during a blackout, the damage is no longer just lost production: it is committed equipment and risk to the staff.
