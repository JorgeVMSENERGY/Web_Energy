How to Select Electrical Cable for Industrial Projects: Ampacity, XLPE vs EPR, IEC 60502 and NMX-J

VMS Energy August 7, 2026 14 min read EPC Technical Supply

Power cable is the most underrated component in industrial EPC projects. It represents between 8 and 15% of the total cost of the electrical installation, but its selection is frequently delegated to a table of standard gauges without validating the three criteria that the standard requires simultaneously: Ampacity, Voltage drop and Short circuit current. A cable selected only for rated current can overheat in final installation, cause unwanted tripping of protections, or fail catastrophically under fault.

This guide presents the complete methodology that VMS Energy applies in industrial EPC projects in Mexico for the selection of power cables, with a focus on medium voltage (1–36 kV) and low critical voltage, including the IEC 60502, NMX-J and NOM-001-SEDE-2012 regulatory framework.

1. The three simultaneous selection criteria

The IEC 60364-5-52 and NOM-001-SEDE-2012 standard establish that the conductor section must satisfy all three criteria at once. The most restrictive determines the final section:

2. Ampacity: real thermal capacity in the field

The Ampacity is the maximum direct current a cable can drive without exceeding its maximum operating temperature. Manufacturers publish it under standard reference conditions; in the field, correction factors should be applied that often reduce capacity by 15 to 40%.

For XLPE the maximum operating temperature is 90°C and the permissible short circuit temperature is 250°C. For PVC it ​​is 70°C in operation and 160°C in short circuit — which explains why PVC is excluded from medium voltage and applications with high short circuit current density.

3. XLPE vs EPR: when to use each insulation

In medium voltage (1–36 kV) the two dominant insulations are XLPE and EPR. Both withstand 90°C operation and 250°C short circuit, but differ in their dielectric and mechanical properties:

CYPE — Reticulated polyethylene

< 0.0004 Lower capacitance IEC standard

Better dielectric behavior in long lines (lower capacitive current). Lower dissipation factor → lower dielectric losses. Dielectric strength ~20 kV/mm. Cheaper per meter.

Applications: Substations, fixed feeders, transmission lines, CFE interconnection, permanent industrial installations.

EPR — Ethylene-propylene

Greater flexibility Resistance to water treeing Mining and oil & gas

Higher mechanical resistance to repeated flexion. Better behaviour in humid environments and at risk of water treeing. Higher dissipation factor (as ~0.003–0.005) but irrelevant in short installations.

Applications: Trailing cables in mining, powering mobile equipment, water environments in permanent contact, marine platforms.

4. Voltage drop calculation

Voltage drop determines the quality of the power at the point of use. For balanced three-phase the general formula is:

Three-phase voltage drop (%) ΔV (%) = [s=3 × I × L × (R·cos ~ + X·sin ~)] / V_n × I = load current (A) · L = circuit length (km) · R = conductor resistance at 90°C (Ω/km) · X = inductive reactance (Ω/km) · cos φ = power factor · V_n = nominal voltage (V)

Resistance R and reactance X values ​​per kilometer are obtained from the manufacturer's data sheet. For XLPE 150 mm² Cu cable at 90°C: R ≈ 0.128 Ω/km; X ≈ 0.082 Ω/km at 13.8 kV.

Limits NOM-001-SEDE-2012: maximum 3% in feeders; maximum 3% in derived circuits; maximum 5% total accumulated. For sensitive loads (variable frequency drives, PLC, SCADA) VMS Energy recommends not to exceed 2% in feeder.

5. Short circuit current verification

The conductor section must thermally withstand the energy of the short circuit during the protection activation time. According to IEC 60949:

Minimum section for short circuit (IEC 60949) S_min (mm²) = I_cc (kA) × √t_cc (s) / k k = 143 for MLPE-copper · k = 94 for MLpe-aluminium · k= 115 for PVC-copper

A differential reliever operating in 0.05 s with I_cc = 10 kA requires S_min = 10,000 × ~0.05 / 143 = 15.6 mm². An MCCB feeder with trip time of 0.5 s with the same short circuit level requires S_min = 49.4 mm² — almost four times as long as protection is to be implemented.

7. Five common mistakes in EPC cable selection

Error 1 — Sizing only for ampacity without verifying voltage drop

In feeders longer than 200 m, the voltage drop frequently requires a larger section than that necessary for ampacity. A 70 mm² cable may be thermally sufficient for 180 A, but at 400 m at 480 V and cos φ = 0.85 it produces ΔV = 4.2% — violating the 3% limit of NOM-001.

Calculate ΔV before confirming the section.

Error 2 — Not applying correction factors for temperature and clustering

Using the manufacturer's table ampacity without applying k_T and k_A can result in a subcalibrated cable. In Monterrey at 40°C with 4 grouped circuits, the actual ampacity can be up to 35% lower than the nominal.

Document in the calculation memory.

Error 3 — Specifying XLPE where EPR is needed (mobile equipment and wet areas)

Error 4 — Ignore the protection trip time in the short circuit calculation

The formula S_min = I_cc × √t / k shows that doubling the actuation time increases the minimum section by √2. A 0.5 s reverse curve overcurrent relay requires significantly larger cross sections than a 0.05 s differential with the same fault level.

Error 5 — Using NMX-J when the project requires IEC 60502 (CFE/LAPEM)

National cables under NMX-J are certified for the domestic market, but do not have the IEC 60502-2 type testing certificate that CFE-LAPEM requires for interconnection. Presenting an NMX-J cable in a LAPEM specification forces it to be replaced, with a direct impact on the schedule.

For CFE projects, always request cable with IEC certification and current type tests.

The XLPE 150 mm² Cu 8.7/15 kV cable with armor, installed directly buried at 0.9 m, meets the three criteria simultaneously with positive margin in all. The most restrictive criterion in this case was the Voltage drop — a 95 mm2 section would have been sufficient for ampness and short circuit, but would have produced ΔV = 2.8% (valid) vs 1.8% for 150 mm2. VMS Energy chose 150 mm2 for the 30% load expansion margin expected by the customer for the second phase.

9. Frequently asked questions

What are the three criteria for selecting an industrial electric cable?

All industrial cables must be verified against three simultaneous criteria: (1) Ampacity — the operating current must not exceed the corrected thermal capacity of the cable (IEC 60287); (2) Voltage drop — the ΔV at the receiving end must not exceed 3% in feeders or 5% in total (NOM-001-SEDE-2012); (3) Short circuit current — the section must withstand the thermal energy of the short circuit without damage (IEC 60949). The most restrictive criterion determines the final section.

What is the difference between the LPE and the RPR insulation in half voltage?

The LCA offers lower dielectric dissipation factor (as - < 0.0004), less capacitance and better behaviour in long lines. It is the standard for fixed substations and installations. EPR is superior in mechanical flexibility, water resistance and extreme humidity environments — ideal for Fryking cables in mining and mobile equipment. Both support 90°C in operation and 250°C in short circuit.

How is the ampacity of a wire buried according to IEC 60287 calculated?

Starting from the manufacturer's nominal ampacity, three correction factors are applied: k_T (ambient ground temperature), k_A (circuit grouping) and k_ρ (ground thermal resistivity). In Mexico, conditions in hot areas or with dry sandy soil can reduce ampacity by up to 35% compared to the table value. VMS Energy prepares the ampacity calculation report as a basic engineering deliverable.

What is the voltage drop limit according to NOM-001-SEDE-2012?

NOM-001-SEDE-2012 establishes a maximum of 3% voltage drop in feeders and 3% in branch circuits, with a cumulative total of no more than 5% from the main panel to the furthest point of use. For sensitive loads such as variable frequency drives, PLC or analytical instrumentation, VMS Energy recommends not exceeding 2% at the feeder.

When is aluminium conductor used instead of copper in industrial projects?

Aluminum is used when the cost of the conductor is a decisive factor and there is space available in trays and ducts for the largest section required (approximately 1.5 times the section of copper for equal ampacity, according to IEC 60228). It is common in long-distance main feeders and high-power connections. For control, instrumentation and small caliber circuits, copper is always maintained.

What standard does it apply for power cables in CFE-specific projects?

For projects with interconnection or CFE specification, IEC 60502-2 (cables from 6 kV to 30 kV) and IEC 62067 (EHV cables greater than 30 kV) are applied, together with the CFE-LAPEM documentation requirements: type and individual test certificate, approved technical sheet and validated installation procedure. In internal distribution projects without CFE requirement, NMX-J and NOM-001-SEDE are sufficient for low voltage.

What documentation should the manufacturer deliver with each cable coil?

The standard documentary package includes: certificate of type tests (tests on sample per batch: withstand voltage, partial discharges, bending), certificate of individual routine tests (DC resistance of the conductor, applied voltage test), cable data sheet (construction, materials, electrical parameters), length report measured per coil and traceability label. For EPC projects with PEMEX, CFE or ISO audit, VMS Energy manages the additional documentation required.
