The challenge: keeping the mine dry, 900 meters underground

In underground mining, water never stops entering. In the deepest levels of the exploitation it accumulates constantly, and if it is not evacuated at the same rate at which it arrives, it floods the productive fronts. That's why he pumping in underground mining is not a support service: it is the condition for everything else to operate.

The Durango operation lifted constant volumes of water from the deepest level to the surface, but it did so with direct startup. This scheme had four chained problems:

  • Permanent operational flooding: continuous risk of losing productive levels by accumulation of water on the deepest fronts.
  • Torque and current peaks: Each direct start hit the engine, the bomb and the mine's power grid.
  • Risk of forced unemployment: a failure of the pumping system forced the complete mine to be stopped until the drain was restored.
  • High energy consumption: pumping at fixed speed, without being able to modulate the power according to the actual demand of each shift.
In underground mining, drainage is not support: it is the condition for everything else to operate.— Engineering equipment · VMS Energy

Why drainage defines the productivity of a mine

At 900 meters deep, the drain becomes the single point of failure for the entire operation. If the pump stops, it is not just one machine that stops: the entire mine stops, because the water rises and makes the exploitation faces impassable. Every minute without pumping is lost production and, in the worst case, compromised assets.

That's where the type of drive changes everything. A direct start turns on the pump at full load suddenly: current spike, torque surge and repeated mechanical stress on equipment that works submerged and is expensive to replace. A frequency changer (VFD) replaces this stroke with a controlled ramp and, in addition, allows the speed of the pump to be adjusted to the real amount of water that must be moved at all times.

For a mine, that translates into three concrete things: less risk of downtime, assets that last longer, and energy that is spent based on demand, not a fixed rate.

The solution: 700 HP Altivar Process 980, integrated by VMS Energy

VMS Energy delivered, configured and launched a frequency changer Schneider Electric Altivar Process 980 700 HP, dedicated to the control of the submersible pump that moves the water column of 900 meters. The unit was fully integrated with the mine's electrical system and with 24/7 monitoring protocols.

The Altivar Process 980 is Schneider Electric's premium family of drives for continuous, heavy and critical processes. VMS Energy—as an authorized integrator for Schneider Electric—selected, sized and commissioned the unit specifically for Durango's deep pumping regime. It wasn't installing equipment: it was designing the drive for the exact conditions of that mine.

Results: immediate impact on the operation

The implementation of the Altivar Process 980 transformed the drainage system: it eliminated electrical peaks, stabilized the flow from the deepest level and reorganized energy consumption around real demand.

  • 24/7 continuous operation: uninterrupted drainage from 900 m, without stoppages attributable to the system.
  • Zero electrical stress spikes: Soft start protects motor, pump and net.
  • Longer useful life of assets: The modulated regime prolongs pump and motor life and reduces replacement costs.
  • Energy adjusted to demand: consumption is odulated with the real need for pumping in each shift and season.
  • Reduced operational risk: Native protections and continuous monitoring prevent failures before they climb to stop.

What this means for your mining operation

The Durango case shows a principle that applies to any operation with critical pumping or heavy rotating equipment: the correct drive not only moves the equipment, it protects business continuity. When the drain is the single point of failure, investing in a sized and well-integrated drive is no longer an equipment expense and becomes production insurance.

And there is a second reading: the difference is not only in the variator, but in who selects it, loChra and commissions it for the actual conditions of the mine. An integrator that responds throughout the system — from the network to the pump — prevents reliability from being lost among suppliers.

Frequently asked questions

Why use a frequency variator for pumping in underground mining?

Because it replaces the direct start—which generates current and torque peaks—with a controlled ramp, and allows the pump speed to be adjusted to the actual drainage demand. This reduces stress on the equipment, prolongs the useful life of the pump and motor, and modulates energy consumption according to each shift.

What is the Altivar Process 980 and why was it chosen for this mine?

It is Schneider Electric's premium family of drives for continuous, heavy and critical processes. VMS Energy selected and sized a 700 HP unit specifically for Durango's deep pumping regime, lifting a 900 meter water column in 24/7 operation.

Does VMS Energy only supply the variator or integrate the entire system?

VMS Energy, as Schneider Electric's authorized integrator, performs the supply, configuration, integration with the mine's electrical system and commissioning, as well as leaving the monitoring protocols active. One responsible for the entire drive.
