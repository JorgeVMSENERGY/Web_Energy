How to size an industrial UPS: IEC 62040-3 topologies, autonomy and battery bank selection

The document that defines whether your critical process survives a 2-second or 4-hour network failure — and why choosing the wrong topology exposes it anyway.

In an industrial plant, there are two types of electrical interruptions: those that the process can absorb without consequences — and those that cost millions. The difference between one and the other is not made by the reliability of CFE: it is made by the supporting engineering. A poorly sized UPS can fail at exactly the moment when it is most needed: when the network fails during a start-up of heavy machinery that generates a voltage drop that the UPS cannot absorb, or when the autonomy calculated under laboratory conditions is not achieved because the battery bank was never parameterized to the actual temperature of the electrical room.

1. The three UPS topologies according to IEC 62040-3: VFI, VI and VFD

The norm IEC 62040-3 is the international standard that classifies the UPS according to how they respond to voltage and frequency variations in the input network. This classification is more accurate — and more useful for project engineering — than the commercial terms "online", "interactive" and "standby", which manufacturers use in a manner that is .

IEC 62040-3

VFIVoltage & I.S.L.

Online double conversion. The load is powered 100% of the time from the inverter: the grid charges the batteries and the inverter powers the load. Transfer time: 0ms. Total galvanic isolation. Regulates voltage and frequency independently of the network. Efficiency: 92–96%.

Application: Oil & Gas, mining, critical control rooms, hospitals, telecommunications.

IEC 62040-3

VIVoltage Independent

Interactive / Line-interactive. The grid feeds the load directly through a regulator (AVR/transformer). The batteries only come in when the grid fails. Transfer time: 4–12 ms (within the window tolerated by IT equipment according to CBEMA, but problematic for sensitive industrial loads).

Application: offices, non-critical IT charges, small data centers in stable environments.

IEC 62040-3

VFDVoltage & I.V.A.

Standby / ffd. The load is connected directly to the grid; The inverter only acts when the grid voltage falls out of range. Transfer time: 10–25 ms. No voltage or frequency regulation in normal operation. Without galvanic isolation.

Application: basic back up in offices, PCs, environments without sensitive loads.

Frequent error: specify an "Ops" "Opks" without verifying that the manufacturer's standard corresponds to VFI topology according to IEC 62040-3. Some manufacturers call "Opps" systems VI with transfer times of 4–8 ms which do not guarantee zero interruption. For industrial projects, always request the IEC 62040-3 classification explicit in the equipment's specification.

2. Step 1 — Inventory and classification of critical loads

Before sizing the UPS, it is essential to separate the loads of the plant's electrical system into three categories:

Category Typical examples Consequence of interruption Requires UPS
Critical loads A DCS, PLC, SCADA, HMI, security systems (ESD/SIS), communications, telecommunications UPS Loss of process control, safety risk, uncontrolled emergency stop Required VFI
Critical loads B Compressor sealing pumps, lubrication systems, transformer cooling, emergency lighting Mechanical damage to rotating equipment, prolonged startup time VFI or generator (depending on response time)
Non-critical loads General lighting, office HVAC, auxiliary workshop equipment Disruption of comfort, not production Does not require UPS; sufficient generator

The inventory must record for each critical load A and B: rated power (kVA), power factor (cosφ), real active power (kW = kVA × cosφ), sensitivity to micro-cuts (ms), and whether it generates harmonics — variable frequency drives and inverters inject THDi that can saturate the internal UPS transformer if not correctly specified.

UPS power required P_UPS (kVA) = Σ P_cargas (kVA) × FSD × 1.20 FSD = Demand Simultaneity Factor (typical 0.80–0.90 for mixed industrial loads). The 1.20 factor is the standard reserve margin to allow for future growth and protect the life of the UPS's internal transformer.

3. Step 2 — Calculation of required autonomy

Required autonomy is defined by the plant's business continuity plan (BCP), not by what "looks reasonable." The correct questions are:

  • How long does it take the emergency generator to start and stabilize? — Typically 10–30 s. The UPS must cover that typn plus a safety margin. Minimum autonomy: 5–15 minutes.
  • What if the generator doesn't start? — The UPS must give time for a secure process . Typical autonomy: 30–60 minutes.
  • Does the site operate in a low CFE reliability area? — In remote areas of oil & gas or mining, 2–8 hour interruptions are possible. 2–8 hours (here the BESS complements the UPS).
Battery bank capacity for target autonomy C_bank (kWh) = P_load (kW) × t_autonomy (h) / η_inverter η_inverter in modern VFI UPS systems (Borri E3001): 94–96%. | Example: 40 kW of critical loads, target backup time 60 min, η = 0.95 → C_bank = 40 × 1.0 / 0.95 = 42.1 kWh.
Correction for temperature: The capacity of the VRLA batteries is significantly reduced with temperature. At 25 °C the capacity is nominal (100%). At 35 °C: ~88%. At 40 °C : ~79%. If the electric room does not have HVAC or exceeds 30 °C as usual, the bench must be oversized to compensate, or migrate to LFP batteries that are much less sensitive to temperature (operational range -20 °C to +60 °C without degradation severe capacity).

4. Step 3 — Battery bank selection: LFP vs VRLA

Battery bank chemistry is the decision that most impacts the total cost of ownership (TCO) of the UPS over its useful life. It's not just the initial price — it's how many years it lasts and how much it costs to replace it.

VRLA / AGM (Plome-Acido Sealed)

  • Life cycles: 500–1000 cycles (depth 80%)
  • Life in flotation: 5–10 years at 20°C, 3–5 years at 30°C
  • Energy density: 30–50 Wh/kg
  • Optimal temperature: 20–25 °C (heat sensitive)
  • Initial cost: Low ($100–180 USD/kWh)
  • Maintenance: Terminal review, semi-annual impedance test
  • Cost per cycle: High in frequent cycling applications
  • Best use case: Pure backrest with <50 cycles/year in heated room

LFP — LiFePO₄ (Lithium Ferrophosphate)

  • Life cycles: ≥8 000 cycles (Geolis certified UL9540A)
  • Life expectancy: 15–20 years with minimal degradation
  • Energy density: 90–160 Wh/kg
  • Operating temperature: -20 °C to +60 °C without severe degradation
  • Initial cost: Major ($300–500 USD/kWh)
  • Maintenance: Minimum — Integrated BMS manages switchgear balancing
  • Cost per cycle: Very low (ideal for BESS with daily cycles)
  • Best use case: BESS with daily peak shaving, hot areas, places without HVAC in the battery room
Parameter VRLA/AGM LFP (LiFePO₄) Advantage
Life cycles (DoD 80%) 500 – 1 000 ≥ 8 000 LFP ×8–16
Life in flotation 5–10 years (at 20°C) 15–20 years LFP ×2
Energy density (Wh/kg) 30–50 90–160 LFP ×3
Heat sensitivity High (–50% at 40 °C) Low (operates –20 to +60 °C) LFP
Initial cost (USD/kWh) $100–180 $300–500 VRLA
Total cost of ownership (10 years) High (2–3 bench replacements) Low (no replacement in 10 years) LFP
Security H₂ gas in overload (ventilation) No gas, no risk of leakage LFP

5. Step 4 — When to add a BESS to the ecosystem

The UPS protects critical control loads for seconds or minutes. The BESS acts at the scale of the plant's electrical system for hours — and with economic objectives, not just backup. They are complementary systems, not substitutes.

Use case 1

Peak shaving — savings on CFE rate

Reduce the maximum demand recorded on the meter. HM/HSL rates include charges of $300–500/kW/month. A 150 kW BESS eliminating 30 minute peaks saves $45,000–75,000/month. Typical ROI: 18–36 months.

Use case 2

Long-lasting backup

In areas with low CFE reliability (remote Oil & Gas, mining) where outages last 2–8 hours. The BESS replaces or complements the generator for non-critical loads, reducing diesel consumption.

Use case 3

Solar + BESS integration

Store the photovoltaic surplus from midday for use in the evening. With Livoltek + Geolis inverters, the solar self-consumption rate exceeds 85% — without any introduction into the network.

Use case 4

Frequency regulation

In microgrids or facilities with their own generation (cogeneration, solar, generators), the BESS cushions frequency transients that the generator cannot follow with sufficient response speed (<100 ms).

Recommended architecture for critical industrial facilities: UPS VFI Borri (zero ms transfer) for control and security charges → BESS Geolis (backrest and peak train) for general electrical system → Emergency generator (unlimited autonomy while fuel is available). All three levels are integrated into the plant SCADA for centralized monitoring and automated energy management strategies.

7. Case: Oil & Gas operational base — Paraíso, Tabasco

Documented EPC case

Oil & Gas operational base — Paraíso, Tabasco (coastal area, high humidity)

Description: Installation of electrical backup for operational offices and control room of a hydrocarbon production field. Area with ambient temperature of 32–38 °C, average RH 85%, CFE interruption history of 4–8 h/month. No pre-installed emergency generator.

Critical load inventory (Category A):

  • SCADA system + historian servers: 8 kVA, cos= 0.85 → 6.8 kW
  • Switches de red y comunicaciones: 2 kVA, cosφ = 0.90 → 1.8 kW
  • Pantallas HMI (sala de control): 3 kVA, cosφ = 0.85 → 2.55 kW
  • Security and access system: 1.5 kVA, cos= 0.90 → 1.35 kW
  • Control room emergency lighting: 1 kW
  • Total A-loads: 15.5 kVA / 13.5 kW

Non-critical loads (Category C) — without UPS backup: Office HVAC (80 kW), general lighting, dining room.

Topology decision: UPS VFI (IEC 62040-3) — mandatory due to SCADA presence and ESD security system.

UPS sizing:

  • Total load: 15.5 kVA × FSD 0.85 = 13.2 kVA actual simultaneous
  • Margen de crecimiento 20%: 13.2 × 1.20 = 15.8 kVA → UPS Borri E2001 de 20 kVA seleccionado
  • Output power factor: 0.9 → active power: 18 kW

Battery bank sizing:

  • Target battery life: 4 hours (covers history of most frequent interruptions)
  • Charging power: 13.5 kW actual
  • C_banco = 13.5 kW × 4 h / 0.95 = 56.8 kWh
  • Ambient temperature: 35 °C average → correction factor VRLA: 0.82
  • If VRLA: C_bank_corrected = 56.8 / 0.82 = 69.3 kWh — very large bench for room without HVAC
  • Decision: LFP bank — without temperature degradation, 56.8 kWh nominal sufficient, 15+ years life vs 3–5 VRLA at 35 °C

Complementary BESS for non-critical loads and peak shaving:

  • Model: Geolis GOC125-241 — 125 kW / 241 kWh, LFP switchgear, IP66, C5 anticorrosive finish
  • Function 1: General lighting and office communications backup during CFE outages (up to 4 hrs at 50 kW demand)
  • Function 2: peak shaving — reduction of peak HVAC demand during peak load hours (14:00 p.m.–19:00 p.m.)
  • IP66 protection and C5 finish — validated for the marine/coastal environment of Tabasco
  • Integration: Modbus TCP to field SCADA, SoC, temperature and cycle monitoring in real time

Result: The installation achieves 4 hours of total autonomy for critical control loads and 2 hours for general loads, covering 95% of the site's CFE outage history without the need to start a generator. BESS peak shaving reduces the maximum registered demand by 90 kW, generating an estimated savings of $36,000/month in HM tariff.

8. Frequently asked questions

What topology does IEC 62040-3 recommend for critical industrial installations?

The only topology suitable for critical industrial processes is VFI (Voltage and I.S. — double online conversion. It provides zero transfer time, total galvanic insulation between network and load, and voltage and frequency regulation completely independent of network quality. The VI (interactive) and VFD (standby) topologies have transfer times of 4–25 ms which can affect control equipment, PCLs and industrial safety systems.

How is the autonomy time of a battery bank calculated for the UPS?

The formula is: t (h) = C_banco (kWh) × η_inversor / P_carga (kW). The load power in kW is obtained from the apparent power in kVA multiplied by the cosφ of the loads. The inverter efficiency in modern VFI UPSs like Borri is typically 94–96%. For VRLA batteries, the electrical room temperature correction factor must additionally be applied — at 35 °C VRLAs deliver only 82% of their nominal capacity.

What is the difference between LFP and VRLA batteries for an industrial UPS?

The VRLA (sealed lead-acid) has low initial cost but shelf life of 3–5 years at 30 °C and is very sensitive to heat. The LFP (lithium ferrophosphate) has a higher initial cost but ≥8,000 life cycles, operates without severe degradation from -20 °C to +60 °C and does not require water maintenance. For applications in non-HVAC rooms, hot coastal areas, or BESS systems with daily cycles, the LFP has a significantly lower total cost of ownership (TCO) over 10 years.

What rules apply to the installation of an industrial system in Mexico?

The main ones are: IEC 62040-1 (UPS security), IEC 62040-3 (topology classification), IEC 61427-2 (stationary batteries), UL 9540 / UL 95 40A (BESS system safety and thermal spread test), NFPA 855 (energy storage facility) and NOM-001-SEDE-2012 for electrical installation in Mexico. VMS Energy Geolis systems are certified under UL9540 and UL95 40A.

When is a BESS justified instead of just a backup generator?

The BESS responds in Milliseconds without start time, it is quiet, without emissions and with minimal maintenance. The generator offers unlimited autonomy but needs 10–30 s to start and stabilize. optimal architecture For critical installations is BESS + generator: the BESS covers the generator starter tyre and all microcuts (<2 s); the generator provides long range if the break exceeds the BESS capacity. A BESS only (without generator) applies when historical interruptions are short (<4 h) and the site does not justify the operating cost of a generator.

What is the "Corque" and how is the BESS for that use case?

Peak shaving consists of downloading the BESS during demand peaks to reduce the maximum recorded in the CFE meter. To size: (1) analyze the demand curve in 15 min windows for 30 days; (2) identify the demand delta to cut (kW) and the typical duration of the peaks; (3) The minimum energy of the BESS is: E = P_clip (kW) × peak_duration (h). With CFE charges of $300–500/kW/month, typical ROI is 18–36 months.

How does an UPS Borri integrate with a BSS Geolis into an EPC project?

They are complementary with different roles: the UPS Borri protects control loads (DCS, SCADA, ESD) with zero more transfer and total galvanic insulation. BESS Geolis It acts on a scale of the plant's electrical system: the "Making" and "Long-Term Support" and solar integration. In VMS Energy projects, both integrate into the main distribution board and SCADA through Modbus TCP or DNP3 for centralized monitoring and coordinated energy management strategies.
