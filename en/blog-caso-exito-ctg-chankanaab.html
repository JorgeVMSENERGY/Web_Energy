The challenge: a MCC that adapts to the island, not the other way around

The CTG Chankanaab is a CFE turbogas plant located on the island of Cozumel. A site like this imposes conditions that standard equipment does not resolve: high humidity, limited physical spaces and the specific CFE standards and requirements. Added to this was a critical restriction: a very short supply time.

The assignment was to design and manufacture a solution for Motor Control Centers (MCC) to adapt to all of the above — not a generic catalogue, but a team designed for this environment, those weights and dimensions, and that regulation.

  • 480 VAC Motor Control Center (alternating current).
  • Motor control center on CD (direct current).
  • Compliance with CFE regulations.

What is a engine control centre and why design matters

An engine control center (MCC) is the cabinet that groups, protects and controls the starters of several engines from one point. It concentrates on the same board the protections, control and distribution of energy towards each engine, which facilitates the operation, maintenance and safety of the entire plant.

Precisely for this reason, a MCC is not a shelf product. Its design depends on the site: the humidity level defines the type of cabinet and protections; The available space determines the dimensions and weight; and the client's regulations—in this case the CFE regulations—condition materials, distances and manufacturing criteria. A well-designed MCC integrates into the environment; A poorly sized one becomes a maintenance and risk problem.

In a generation plant, this board is part of the electrical backbone: whether the process motors continue to operate depends on its robustness and reliability.

The solution: two robust, safe and easy-to-maintain systems

The VMS Energy engineering team developed a solution tailored to the environment and the client's demanding regulations, solving the three site constraints: high humidity conditions, the dimensions adjusted to limited spaces and very short supply time.

The design started from a clear premise: to provide two systems that were robust, easy to maintain, safe, resistant and reliable. On that basis, VMS Energy was responsible for the engineering and manufacturing, supply, installation, commissioning and commissioning of the CCMs and low voltage distribution boards.

Why it is worth updating your electrical panels

A distribution board divides the power supply into derivative circuits and protects each one within a common cabinet: it is an essential component of any installation. Keeping it up to date — or designing it well from the start, as in Chankanaab — directly impacts the operation:

SecurityProtection of people and equipment against electrical failures.
Energy efficiencyLess losses and better use of distributed energy.
Regulatory complianceDesign in accordance with applicable standards, such as those of CFE.
Capacity and expansionSpace and architecture to grow without redoing the entire board.
Reliability and continuityFewer unplanned stoppages and more stable operations.
Diagnosis and monitoringVisibility of the system state to anticipate failures.

And, above all, long-term cost reduction: A well-designed board costs less in maintenance, downtime and replacement over its entire useful life.

What this means for your operation

The Chankanaab case leaves a lesson that applies to any generation plant or installation with demanding conditions: the correct panel or MCC is not the cheapest one in the catalog, but the one that is designed for the real site — its climate, its space and its regulations. When the environment is hostile (an island, high humidity, limited space), custom design is what separates equipment that lasts from one that becomes a liability.

  • The site commands: humidity, space and customer standards define the design, not the other way around.
  • Own manufacturing allows very short supply times without sacrificing quality.
  • One person responsible —from engineering and manufacturing to commissioning — ensures that equipment arrives, enters and operates.

Frequently asked questions

What is an engine control center (MCC)?

It is the cabinet that groups, protects and controls the starters of several motors from a single point. It concentrates protections, control and energy distribution towards each motor, which facilitates the operation, maintenance and safety of the plant.

Why should a MCC be tailored to the site?

Because the environment defines the equipment: the humidity level determines the type of cabinet and protections, the available space determines dimensions and weight, and the client's regulations (for example, CFE standards) determine materials and manufacturing criteria. At CTG Chankanaab, in Cozumel, these conditions were especially demanding.

What did VMS Energy deliver at the Chankanaab CTG?

VMS Energy designed, muttered, supplied, installed, commissioned and launched two engine control centers (one of a total of 480 VAC and one of a direct current) and low voltage distribution boards, in accordance with CFE standards and in a very short supply time.
