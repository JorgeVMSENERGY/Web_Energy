DEEMSA, WEG, ABB, Siemens and Schneider: guide to choosing the right transformer manufacturer for your project

There is no universal best brand. There is the right specification — and the manufacturer that best meets it for your project.

One of the questions we most often receive when starting a project is “What brand of transformer does it recommend?” The correct answer always puzzles us a little: depends. Not because we want to evade the question, but because choosing the manufacturer before defining the technical specification is putting the cart before the horse.

This article documents the analysis framework that VMS Energy applies in its EPC projects to evaluate and recommend transformer manufacturers. The goal is not to create a brand classification: it is to give the engineer or procurement manager the tools to make the right decision based on their specific project.

1. Criteria that determine the selection — not the brand

Before comparing manufacturers, there are five dimensions of the project that limit the universe of valid options:

Primary voltage level

The most decisive filter

Distribution (up to 34.5 kV): DEEMSA, WEG, Schneider. High voltage (69 kV+): WEG, ABB/Hitachi Energy, Siemens. Extra-high voltage (230 kV+): ABB/Hitachi Energy, Siemens.

Project schedule

Actual delivery time

National or regional manufacturers (DEEMSA, WEG): 4–14 weeks. Global manufacturers (ABB, Siemens): 16–36 weeks. A tight schedule may eliminate options before evaluating price.

Type of isolation

Oil vs. dry

Fire-restricted interiors (buildings, data centers, hospitals): dry transformer — Schneider, ABB, WEG. Outdoor or semi-outdoor, higher powers: mineral oil — all manufacturers.

Integration requirements

SCADA, OLTC, protocol

Projects with under load tap changer (OLTC) integrated with SCADA or with IEC 61850 protocols: ABB/Hitachi Energy and Siemens have an advantage in this segment due to their automation ecosystem.

EPC Principle: The technical data sheet (data sheet) with the mandatory parameters must be issued before requesting a quote. The manufacturer responds to a specification—not defines it. If an offer proposes to change the vector group, the Zcc or the type of cooling “because the manufacturer has them as standard”, the offer must be rejected or accurately evaluated whether the change is technically neutral.

2. DEEMSA — national reference manufacturer in distribution

MX

DEEMSA

Mexican manufacturer · Monterrey, N.L. · Distribution in oil and dry up to ~10 MVA

Key strengths

  • National manufacturer with a plant in Mexico: fast local technical support
  • Short delivery times (4–8 weeks in standard distribution)
  • Direct compliance with NMX-J-116-ANCE and IEC 60076
  • Competitive prices in oil distribution up to 5 MVA
  • Availability of spare parts and spare parts in the national market
  • Knowledge of the CFE market and Mexican industrial projects

Limitations to consider

  • Portfolio limited to distribution (LV and MV up to ~34.5 kV)
  • No presence in HV power transformers (≥ 69 kV)
  • Less documentation of type testing vs. global manufacturers
  • Smaller dry transformer portfolio than Schneider or ABB
Optimal scenario: Industrial LV/MV oil distribution projects up to 5 MVA, with tight schedule or need for local support in Mexico. Manufacturing plants, agribusiness, municipal infrastructure.

3. WEG — broad portfolio with strong regional presence

BR

WEG

Brazilian manufacturer · Regional presence in Mexico · Distribution and power up to 500+ MVA

Key strengths

  • Portfolio that covers from distribution (LV) to HV and extra-HV power
  • Distribution and technical support network established in Mexico
  • Intermediate and competitive delivery times (8–16 weeks)
  • Good price-performance ratio in distribution and average power
  • Integrator in projects that include motors, drives and transformers WEG
  • Compliance with IEC 60076, IEEE C57 and Latin American standards

Limitations to consider

  • In large-scale HV/extra-HV, ABB and Siemens have greater track record in Mexico
  • Field support for HV power equipment may have longer response times than manufacturers with their own service centers
  • Automation ecosystem (OLTC, SCADA) less mature than ABB or Siemens in transmission projects
Optimal scenario: Medium scale industrial projects requiring distribution and power up to 69 kV, or projects where the plant already operates with WEG (single integrator economics) motors and drives.

4. ABB / Hitachi Energy — global reference in HV power transformers

H.E.

ABB / Hitachi Energy

Global manufacturer · Presence in Mexico · Distribution, power and extra-HV up to 765 kV

Key strengths

  • World reference in HV and extra-HV power transformers
  • Extensive experience in special designs: auto-transformers, phase transformers, voltage regulators
  • Robust ecosystem of proprietary OLTC (integrated Reinhausen brand), protection and SCADA
  • Compliance with IEC, IEEE, ANSI and CFE transmission standards
  • Global spare parts support and specialized service
  • References in transmission and generation projects in Mexico

Limitations to consider

  • Long delivery times for HV power: 20–36 weeks or more
  • Significantly higher cost than national or regional manufacturers
  • Less competitive in standard distribution (< 5 MVA) where the extra cost is not justified
  • The ABB–Hitachi Energy separation may generate confusion in after-sales support depending on the product
Optimal scenario: HV and extra-HV power transformers (≥ 69 kV), transmission or generation projects, critical infrastructure with advanced OLTC requirements or IEC 61850 integration, and projects where the customer or funder requires a world-class manufacturer.

5. Siemens Energy — system integration and automation

SE

Siemens Energy

Global manufacturer · Presence in Mexico · MV, HV and extra-HV power, SCADA integration

Key strengths

  • Reference in power transformers with native integration to Siemens SCADA systems
  • Complete solution: transformer + MV switchgear + Siprotect protection + automation
  • Strong in energy infrastructure projects: large-scale transmission and distribution substations
  • Experience in markets with CFE and CENACE specifications
  • GEOFOL dry-type distribution transformers with long trajectory

Limitations to consider

  • ABB-like delivery times: 18–32 weeks in HV power
  • High cost; the value proposition is the integrated system, not the transformer as an isolated component
  • In standard distribution, the price is not competitive compared to DEEMSA or WEG
  • Siemens AG — Siemens Energy reorganization may affect commercial support depending on the product
Optimal scenario: Projects where the transformer is part of an integrated Siemens system (switchgear + protection + SCADA), transmission substations, or projects where the client already operates Siemens infrastructure and needs platform continuity.

6. Schneider Electric — dry transformers and medium voltage ecosystem

SCH

Schneider Electric

Global manufacturer · Wide presence in Mexico · Dry transformers and MV distribution

Key strengths

  • Reference on dry type epoxy resin transformers (Trihal, Minera) for interiors
  • Natural integration with SM6 / RM6 MV switchgear and Prisma boards from the same manufacturer
  • High availability in Mexico; extensive distributor network
  • Solutions for data-CdD, smart buildings, hospitals and commerce
  • Energy Management Support (EcoStruxure Power) for Monitoring and Efficiency

Limitations to consider

  • It is not a reference manufacturer of large-scale HV power transformers
  • The dry transformer has a higher initial cost vs. oil for the same power
  • Less competitiveness in heavy industrial projects that require mineral oil and high power
  • Differential value requires adopting the complete Schneider ecosystem to materialize
Optimal scenario: Dry-type transformers for indoors (commercial centers, hospitals, data-cleansing, office buildings, offshore platforms), or projects where MV infrastructure is already Schneider (switchgear + protection + energy management).

8. The right process: specify first, choose manufacturer later

In well-managed EPC projects, manufacturer selection is the result of an engineering process, not its starting point. The process that VMS Energy follows in its procurement projects has four steps:

  1. Define the technical data sheet (data sheet): Power, voltages, vector group, Zcc, cooling type, standards, accessories, site altitude, maximum temperature, insulation type, guaranteed loss values. This sheet is the basis of the technical contract with the manufacturer.
  2. Identify the universe of qualified manufacturers for that specification: voltage level, power, insulation type and project times filter the field to 2–3 real options.
  3. Request technical and commercial offer: Compare not only price, but: guaranteed loss values, delivery times, scope of FAT included, warranty and post-sales support conditions. The TCO (Total Cost of Ownership) evaluation considers the energy consumed in losses throughout the useful life of the equipment.
  4. Award with clear list of deviations: If the manufacturer proposes changes to the specification, each deviation must be technically evaluated and accepted or rejected in writing before issuing the purchase order.
TCO vs. initial price: A 2,000 kVA distribution transformer operates 8,760 hours per year. If the vacuum losses of option A are 2,800 W and those of option B are 3,400 W, the difference is 600 W constants. At a power cost of 1.80 ML/kWh, that difference represents ~9,400 MLN per year. In 25 years of service life: more than 235,000 MLB additional. If option B was 80,000 MLB cheaper, it is still more expensive at the end of the life cycle.

9. Case: selection of manufacturer for 10 MVA substation, Monterrey logistics park

Procurement case — Industrial logistics park, Nuevo León

Power transformer 10 MVA, 115 kV / 13.8 kV, Din11, ONAF, Zcc 7% with OLTC ± 10%

Context: EPC project for electrical substation of logistics park in Nuevo León. The header transformer is 10 MVA with a 115 kV primary (CFE transmission line), with OLTC required to maintain stable voltage in the event of grid variations. Schedule: 22 weeks until first energization. The client requested evaluation from three manufacturers.

Manufacturers evaluated and result:

  • DEEMSA: Not qualified. It does not have a portfolio in 115 kV with OLTC in that power.
  • WEG: Qualified. Offered delivery time: 18 weeks. Price: reference 100. Guaranteed no-load losses: 9,200 W. Confirmed Zcc 7%. OLTC with IEC 60214 protocol. TCO at 25 years: reference 100.
  • ABB/Hitachi Energy: Qualified. Offered delivery time: 28 weeks — exceeds the project schedule by 6 weeks. Price: reference 135. Guaranteed no-load losses: 7,800 W. OLTC with native IEC 61850 intelligent monitoring.

Decision: WEG selected. The ABB option was technically superior (minor losses, more sophisticated OLTC), but delivery time made it impossible to meet the project schedule without penalty to the contractor. ABB's lower losses would have been savings of ~18,000 BDN/year (difference of 1,400 W × 8,760 h × 1.80 BDN), which does not offset the risk of non-compliance with the schedule.

Project learning: Global manufacturers' delivery time for HV power should be quoted and confirmed before making schedule commitments to the end customer. In transmission or high power projects, leaving 30–40 weeks of margin for the specification-quote-manufacturing-delivery process is not conservative: it is the reality of the market.

Frequently asked questions about manufacturer selection

Which is the best manufacturer of transformers for industrial distribution in Mexico?

There is no universal “best” manufacturer. For LV/MV distribution up to 5 MVA with short delivery times, DEEMSA and WEG are competitive options with solid local support. For HV power transformers (≥ 69 kV) or with advanced OLTC, ABB/Hitachi Energy and Siemens are references. The correct rule is: specify first, select the manufacturer later.

Does DEEMSA manufacture high voltage transformers?

DEEMSA is a specialist in oil and dry type distribution transformers up to approximately 10 MVA and 34.5 kV primary. For projects that require HV (69 kV+), DEEMSA is not a reference manufacturer; WEG, ABB/Hitachi Energy or Siemens are the applicable options depending on the voltage level and schedule.

Why is WEG frequent in EPC projects in Mexico?

Due to its combination of broad portfolio (distribution and power), solid regional support network in Mexico, intermediate delivery times (8–16 weeks) and competitive prices compared to leading global manufacturers. In projects where the scope includes WEG motors and drives, the transformer from the same manufacturer also simplifies warranty and integration management.

When is ABB or Siemens justified instead of other brands?

In four scenarios: (1) HV or extra-HV power transformers where these manufacturers have a real advantage in design and references. (2) Projects with advanced OLTC integrated with SCADA (IEC 61850). (3) Critical infrastructure where the client or financier requires a manufacturer with a global presence. (4) Projects where the complete system (transformer + switchgear + protection) is of the same brand and the value is in the integration.

What advantage does Schneider Electric offer in transformers?

Schneider is the reference in dry type epoxy resin transformers (Trihal) for interiors where the risk of oil spills is eliminated: buildings, data centers, hospitals, commerce. Its additional advantage is the integration with MV switchgear (SM6/RM6), LV boards (Prisma) and energy management (EcoStruxure), which reduces coordination complexity when the entire infrastructure is from the same manufacturer.

Can you ask two manufacturers for an offer for the same project?

Yes, and it is the right practice to get EPC. The key is to issue the same technical data sheet to all manufacturers and compare not only the initial price, but the TCO: vacuum and load losses during the life of the equipment, delivery time, warranty, and cost of spare parts and support. A cheaper transformer with higher losses can cost more money in a 10-25-year cycle.

What criteria should a data sheet include to request an offer?

Rated power, primary and secondary voltages, vector group, Zcc (%), type of cooling, applicable standards, type of insulation, site altitude, maximum ambient temperature, tap changer (manual or OLTC, range and steps), required accessories (Buchholz, OTI, WTI, oil level), test standards, and guaranteed maximum values of no-load and load losses.

Need support for specification and search for transformers?

VMS Energy manages the complete process of processing transformers: we produce the data sheet, request offers from qualified manufacturers according to their project, evaluate the TCO and coordinate the FAT. No brand bias: the project specification defines the manufacturer.
