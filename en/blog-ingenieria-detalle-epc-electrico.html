Detail engineering in electrical EPC projects: from the one-line diagram to the IFC package

The phase where 80% of the project's technical decisions are made — and where errors cost more if they are detected late.

In an EPC project, the construction phase is visible: you see the trench, the cable, the substation being assembled. But 80% of the technical decisions that determine whether that facility will operate correctly, safely and efficiently are made weeks in advance — in the detailed engineering phase. An error in the short circuit calculation memory can result in a switch with insufficient breaking capacity. An incomplete one-line diagram can cause poorly coordinated protection that shuts down the entire plant in the event of a minor fault. This article documents how VMS Energy performs electrical detail engineering on industrial EPC projects.

1. The detailed engineering phase in the EPC cycle

Every EPC project (Engin Mapped, Procurement, and Construction) goes through three levels of engineering before it reaches the field. Understanding the difference between them is fundamental to understanding the role and weight of detail engineering:

Level 1

Conceptual engineering

Defines the scope, design bases, required power, voltage levels and general architecture of the system. Produces a concept one-line diagram and class C cost estimate (±30%). Lasts weeks.

Level 2

Basic engineering

Develops the design criteria, defines the main equipment (transformers, switchgear, CCMs), and produces the approved single-line to procure long-term equipment. Allows class B estimation (±15%).

Level 3 — This item

Detail engineering

Transforms basic engineering definitions into IFC documents (Issued for Building): construction plans, verified computational memories, data sheets of all equipment, definitive material listings.

Result

IFC Package

The IFC package is the set of documents that the construction team needs to execute the project without interpreting or improvising. No document can be used in the field without the IFC seal issued by the technical person responsible for the project.

The cost of corrections rule: An error detected in the detail engineering phase costs 1× to correct it. The same error detected during the search costs 10×. Detected during construction: 100×. detected in operation: 1000×. The quality of detail engineering is the highest return investment in any EPC project.

2. The one-line diagram: project master document

The Single Line Diagram (SLD) is the document that describes the complete architecture of the electrical system: power sources, transformers, bars, feeders, protection equipment, measurement and instrumentation, all represented with a single line per three-phase circuit. It is the first document to be prepared and the last to be updated (like As-Built).

Information that an IFC single-wire line must contain

Element Required information Common mistake
Transformers kVA/MVA, voltages, vector group (e.g. Din11), Zcc (%), cooling type, TAPs available Skip vector group — critical for differential protection and grounding
Switches and disconnectors Rated current (A), rated voltage (kV), breaking capacity (kA), type (vacuum, SF₆, air) Do not indicate the breaking capacity — prevents verification of sufficiency against short circuit
Current transformers (TC) Transformation ratio, accuracy class (e.g. 5P20), rated load (VA) Skip the accuracy class — measuring CTs are not used for protection and vice versa
Protection relays Type (51, 50, 87, 67), model, function number ANSI, configuration Only indicate the function without the model — prevents programming parameters in engineering
Bars and feeders Conductor section (mm² or AWG), insulation type, installation method, run length Not indicating the length — prevents checking voltage drop without consulting plans
Grounding Neutral type (solid, impedance, isolated), mesh system connection, target resistance (-) Failure to specify neutral grounding type — affects all ground fault coordination

The single line must reference the symbol standard used: IEC 60617 for projects under European/international standard; or ANSI/IEEE Std 315 For projects with North American influence or NEC. Both are accepted in Mexico, but within the same project a single standard of simbology should be used.

3. Short circuit calculation memory (IEC 60909)

The short-circuit current calculation is the most impactful memory in electrical engineering: it determines the minimum breaking capacity of all switches in the system, the required thermal resistance for conductors and bars, and the maximum settings of overcurrent relays. A switch with insufficient breaking capacity can explode upon failure, with catastrophic consequences.

IEC 60909 method: equivalent impedances

The IEC 60909 standard calculates the maximum initial symmetrical short-circuit current using the method of equivalent impedances referred to the fault bus. For a symmetrical three-phase fault, the short circuit current is:

Three-phase short-circuit current (IEC 60909) I”k3 = (c × VN) / (√3 × |ZTotal|) Where: c = voltage factor (1.0 minimum / 1.05 to 1.1 maximum) — VN = nominal bus voltage [kV] — ZTotal = total circuit impedance referred to the fault bus [Ω]

In practice, the total impedance is constructed by adding the impedances of all elements in series between the source and the failure point: supply network (CFE), transformers, cables, buses. Each element refers to the same voltage level through the transformation ratio.

Numerical example: 480 V industrial substation bus

Calculation example

Bus short circuit current 480 V — transformer 2,000 kVA

System data: Transformer 2,000 kVA, 13.8 kV / 480 V, Dyn11, Zcc = 5.75%. Impedance of the CFE network referring to 13.8 k V: Znetwork = 0.08 + j0.42 Ω (MVAcc = 450 MVA at the connection point).

Step 1 — Base impedance at 480 V:

ZBase = VN² / SN = (0.480)² / 2.0 = 0.1152 Ω

Step 2 — Transformer impedance referred to 480 V:

ZT = Zcc% × ZBase = 0.0575 × 0.1152 = 0.00662 Ω

Step 3 — Grid impedance referred to 480 V:

Znet,480 = Znet,13.8kV × (480/13,800)² → despicable (ZT dominates when the transformer is the only limiting element)

Step 4 — Maximum short circuit current on 480 V bus:

I”k3 = (1.05 × 0.480) / (√3 × 0.00662) = 0.504 / 0.01147 = 43,940 A ≈ 44 kA

Conclusion: All switches on the bus of the bus of 480 V must have breaking capacity ≥ 44 kA. Standard low voltage switches of 18 kA or 22 kA are excluded. Frame switches with breaking capacity of 50 kA or 65 kA are required, or the installation of a short circuit limitation coil (reactor) must be evaluated.

The most expensive mistake in electrical procurement: Specify low voltage switches (NEMA/IEC) without checking the 480 V bus short circuit current. 1,000–3,000 kVA distribution transformers with Zcc of 5–6% generate secondary short circuit currents of 35 to 60 kA. A 22 kA breaker installed on that bus is a catastrophic arc flash risk.

4. Load flow and voltage drop

The load flow analysis verifies that under normal operating conditions and under contingency, the voltage on each bus in the system remains within the allowed range. In industrial facilities, the NOM-001-SEDE-2012 sets the reference limits:

Criterion Limit NOM-001-SEDE Recommended EPC criterion Applies to
Maximum branch circuit drop 3% 2% (sensitive loads) Motor, lighting, branched circuit
Total cumulative drop 5% 4% (industrial projects) Connection + feeder + branch
Drop in control circuits Not specified 2% PLC, instruments, 24 V DC circuits
Transformer voltage regulation Not specified ±5% with available TAPS Transformer MV/LV bus
Drop in engine start Not specified ≤15% transitional Bus during direct start DOL

The voltage drop formula for balanced three-phase circuits is:

Three-phase voltage drop (%) ΔV% = (√3 × I × L × (R × cosφ + X × sinφ)) / (10 × VN) I = load current [A] — L = conductor length [m] — R = conductor resistance [Ω/km] — X = conductor reactance [Ω/km] — VN = line voltage [V] — cosφ = load power factor

In the practice of EPC projects, the voltage drop calculation is carried out using ETAP or using standardized spreadsheets for each section of the system. The result determines the minimum section of the conductor that meets both thermal capacity and voltage drop.

5. Sizing of conductors

The electrical conductor is sized by two independent criteria, and the final section is the largest value that results from both:

Criterion 1

Thermal capacity (ampacity)

Determined by rated load current + ambient temperature correction factors, conductor grouping and conduit type. Regulatory basis: NOM-001-SEDE-2012 Table 310-16 (equivalent to NEC 310.15).

Criterion 2

Voltage drop

Determined by the length of the section and the maximum allowable fall. In installations with long feeders (> 100 m), the thermal criterion usually dominates and results in larger section conductors.

Additionally, drivers must be verified for their short circuit support capacity: The maximum temperature reached by the conductor during the operating time of the upstream switch must not exceed the insulation limit (90°C for normal THHN/THHW, 150°C under short circuit).

Short circuit thermal verification Smin = ICC × √t / k Smin = minimum section [mm²] — ICC = short circuit current [A] — t = switch operating time [s] — k = 143 for copper THW/THHN (initial temperature 75°C)
Example: 480 V bus, ICC = 44,000 A, upstream switch operating time = 0.08 s (60 Hz cycles). Yesmin = 44,000 × √0.08 / 143 = 44,000 × 0.283 / 143 = 87 mm². A 2/0 AWG (67 mm²) conductor does not comply; 3/0 AWG (85 mm²) or 4/0 AWG (107 mm²) minimum is required for this criterion.

6. Load Balance (Load Schedule)

The load balance (or Load Schedule) is the complete inventory of all project loads, organized by panel and feeder. It is the document that allows sizing the transformers, emergency generators, main switches and the total installed capacity of the substation.

Column Description How to obtain
Installed power (kW) Nominal load power according to plate Technical sheet of the equipment or process
Demand factor (DF) Fraction of time operating at full load. Pump motor: 0.80. Lighting: 1.0. Intermittent loads: 0.50–0.65 Production process, customer specification
Maximum demand (kW) Installed power × FD Calculation
Power factor (cosφ) Induction motor: 0.80–0.85. VFD: ≥0.95. LED lighting: 0.90 Technical data sheet / design standards
kVA demand kW / cos Calculation
Matching factor (CF) Probability that all loads operate simultaneously. For continuous processes: 0.90. For general use: 0.70–0.80 Engineering criteria / process data
transformer design kVA Suma de demandas kVA × FC × margen de crecimiento (typically 1.20–1.25) Calculation — basis for transformer selection
The most common error in load balancing: Add all installed power without applying demand and match factors, and Shame the transformer for 100% of the sum. This results in oversized transformers between 30% and 60%, with higher initial cost and inefficient operation (processors operate more efficiently between 50% and 75% of charge). The correct application of the demand factor is not conservatism. excessive: it is engineering.

7. Construction plans: pipes, grounding and lighting

The calculation reports determine the requirements; The construction plans specify how to install them. Three plans are especially critical in any substation or industrial electrical infrastructure project:

Conduit & Cable Tray Plan

Defines the routing of cable trays and conduits, the entry and exit points in equipment, the crossings with other disciplines (pipe, structure), and the conduit filling percentages. The NOM-001-SEDE-2012 states that the area occupied by drivers should not exceed 40% of the interior area of the "Hungry" (one single driving: 53%). The channel plane should be coordinated with the BIM model or the plant plans of other disciplines to avoid interference.

Grounding Plan

The grounding mesh is the fundamental safety system of the substation. Its design follows the standard IEEE Std 80 (Guide for Safety in AC Substation Grounding) and must demonstrate that the tensions of passage and contact during a fault to earth do not exceed the limits tolerable by the human body.

The critical parameters of the mesh design are:

  • Soil resistivity (ρ): It is measured in the field with the Wenner method (IEEE Std 81). In Mexico, typical values ​​range from 30 Ω·m (wet clay soil) to >1,000 Ω·m (rock). Resistivity directly determines step and contact voltages.
  • Mesh resistance (RG): For industrial substations, the typical target is RG ≤ 1 For transmission substations or with high fault currents, the pass/contact or voltage criterion dominates the absolute resistance.
  • Mesh conductor gauge: Minimum 2/0 AWG annealed bare copper (most common in Mexico) or 4/0 AWG in substations with high fault current (> 10 kA for more than 1 second).
  • Ground rods (ground-sands): Copperweld 5/8" × 10 ft, peripheral mesh with rods in corners and every 6–9 m depending on soil resistivity.

Lighting Layout

The design of industrial lighting must comply with the illuminance levels established by the NOM-025-STPS-2008 (Lighting conditions in work centers). For substations and electrical rooms, the minimum level is 200 lux (moderate precision work). The lighting plan must indicate the type of luminaire, the power, the mounting height, the calculated illuminance level (DIALux or AGi32 software) and the emergency lighting circuits (minimum 10 lux for evacuation, NOM-001-SEDE-2012).

9. Software used in electrical engineering EPC

Modern electrical detail engineering is not performed with manual calculations beyond the verification level. Specialized software allows you to analyze complex systems, generate automatic lists of materials and guarantee traceability between the system model and construction plans.

Software Main use Strength in EPC projects Supported standards
ETAP Analysis of electrical systems: short circuit, charge flow, protection coordination, electric arc (Arc Flash) Single data model that powers all studies. Engineering reports directly exportable to IFC IEC 60909, ANSI C37, IEEE 1584 (Arc Flash), IEC 60909
SKM PowerTools Short circuit, load flow, coordination, NEC conductor sizing Greater penetration in NEC projects (USA/North American Mexico). Familiar interface for NEC-trained engineers NEC, ANSI/IEEE C37, IEEE 1584
AutoCAD Electrical Electrical plans and diagrams with automatic generation of bills of materials (BOM), I/O reports and cable numbering Integration with AutoCAD Plant3D and Revit. Collaborative editing with external references (XRef) IEC 60617, ANSI/IEEE 315
DigSILENT PowerFactory Dynamic studies, transient stability, transmission and distributed generation. Standard in utilities and generation projects Maximum precision in transmission studies, grid interconnections and microgrids with renewable generation IEC 60909, IEC 60068, International Grid Codes
Revit MEP / BIM 360 Multidisciplinary coordination in 3D. Interference detection (clash detection) between electrical, HVAC, structure and process Eliminates pre-field installation interference. In large projects it reduces pipeline rework by up to 30% ISO 19650 (BIM)
The relationship between ETAP and AutoCAD Electrical: At VMS Energy, the ETAP model is built first and is the source of truth for all electrical parameters: breaking capacities, conductor sizes, protection settings. The plans in AutoCAD Electrical extract these parameters and reflect them in the diagrams. If the ETAP model changes (for example, because the customer modified a load), the drawings are updated from the source, not manually. This guarantees consistency between calculation reports and construction plans.

10. Case: 5 MVA substation for industrial plant in Coahuila

Engineering case — Steel processing plant, Coahuila

Detail engineering for 115 kV / 13.8 kV, 5 MVA substation, with 8 distribution feeders

Project context: Expansion of a steel processing plant in Coahuila. The newly built substation would power three resistance furnaces, oil cooling system, compressors, industrial lighting and control system. The complexity of the project was in the high non-linear demand of the furnaces (high harmonic content) and the need to guarantee continuity of service for the critical process.

Scope of detailed engineering:

  • 47 IFC documents issued in 7 weeks of engineering
  • ETAP model with 3 MV buses (13.8 kV), 12 LV buses (480 V) and 86 modeled electrical elements
  • Harmonic analysis (IEEE 519): furnaces inject up to 18% 5th harmonic — passive filters were specified on 13.8 kV bus and line reactors on furnace feeders
  • Grounding mesh design: field measured resistivity 68 Ω·m, 850 m² mesh, 4/0 AWG Cu conductor, result: RG = 0.6 Ω

Short circuit calculation result:

  • Bus 13.8 kV: IDC3 = 3.8 kA (transformer dominates — MV switchgear selected 12.5 kA breakdown: ample reserve)
  • Bus 480 V: IDC3 = 31.4 kA (Zcc 5.5% of 5 MVA transformer). Frame switches of 42 kA breaking capacity were required on the entire LV bus
  • Three power supply units with 800 A rated current and 31.4 kA short circuit: the minimum conductor by thermal criterion was 350 MCM, but the voltage drop criterion at 120 m required 500 MCM. 500 MCM THHN/90°C was installed on EGC tray.

Critical design decision — detected in ETAP, not in the field: During the coordination analysis, the model showed that the definite-time overcurrent relay (51) in the main furnace feeder operated before the ground fault relay (51G) of the transformer upon an interphase fault at the furnace terminal. This would have disconnected the furnace due to a fault that had to be contained by the feeder. The time-dial of relay 51 on the feeder was adjusted from 0.1 to 0.2 s to ensure selectivity. The adjustment took 2 hours in engineering and would have taken weeks of field diagnosis if it had been omitted from the study.

Learning: In installations with non-linear loads (furnaces, rectifiers, high-power VFDs), harmonic analysis is not optional: it is an integral part of the detailed engineering. Harmonics cause heating in transformers, failure of power factor correction capacitors and destabilization of overcurrent relays that are not robust to asymmetric peak values.

Frequently asked questions about electrical detail engineering

What documents do electrical detail engineering include in an EPC project?

The minimum package includes a general and detailed single-line diagram per board, short-circuit calculation reports (IEC 60909), load flow and voltage drop, load balance, conductor sizing, list of materials, equipment data sheets, piping plans, grounding, lighting, control diagram and protection coordination table. The complete package is issued in IFC state before starting construction.

What standard is used for short circuit calculation in Mexico?

In industrial projects in Mexico it is mainly used IEC 60909 (impedance method, adopted in medium and high voltage projects) or ANSI/IEEE C37.010 For NEC-standard projects. Tools such as ETAP and SKM PowerTools implement both methods. The result determines the minimum breakability (kA) of all switches and the selection of conductors by thermal resistance to short circuit.

What is the maximum voltage drop allowed in industrial facilities in Mexico?

The NOM-001-SEDE-2012 It establishes 3% in derivative circuit and 5% accumulated total (acometida + feeder + derivative).In industrial EPC practice, more conservative criteria are used: 2% in control circuits and sensitive loads, 3% in force circuits. In installations with long feeders (> 100 m), the voltage drop criterion usually results in conductors of greater section than the thermal criterion.

What is the unify diagram and why is the master document?

The Single Line Diagram represents the complete electrical system using a single line per three-phase circuit, with standardized symbols (IEC 60617 or ANSI/IEEE 315). It is the main reference document for procurement of equipment, coordination of protections, permits and operation. Any changes made during or after construction must be reflected in the single-line, which ends the project as As-Built.

What software is used for EPC electrical engineering?

The most used tools are: ETAP for short circuit, load flow, protection coordination and the arc flash; SKM PowerTools with similar capacities under NEC standard; AutoCAD Electrical for plans and schematics with automatic BOM; DigSILENT PowerFactory for transmission and dynamic static studies; and Revit MEP For multidisciplinary BIM coordination. In VMS Energy, ETAP is the primary data source and AutoCAD Electrical reflects that data in the construction plans.

What is the IFC state of detail engineering?

IFC (Issued for Construction) indicates that the document has completed all internal and client reviews, incorporated all comments, and is authorized to be executed in the field. The previous statuses are IFR (Issued for Review) and IFA (Issued for Approval). Upon completion of the project, the documents are updated with the actual changes executed and issued as As-Built, which is the definitive record of how the facility was built.

How long does the electrical detail engineering phase last?

For a standard 1–5 MVA industrial substation: 4 to 8 weeks. Complex projects (entire plants, HV, multiple substations) require 12 to 20 weeks. The phase is critical because the IFC documents are the input for the procurement of long-term delivery equipment (transformers, MV switchgear, CCMs) whose manufacturing begins while engineering is still active. Delaying detailed engineering cascades the entire procurement and construction chain.
