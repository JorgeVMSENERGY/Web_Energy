The hidden cost

Power quality issues don't show up on the bill — they show up in production

Premature failures in drives, relays that trip without apparent cause, batch rejections in pharmaceutical or food processes, CFE penalties for low power factor. They all have the same origin: energy outside the regulatory limits at the point of coupling.

IEEE 519-2022 / IEC 61000-3-2

Current and voltage harmonics (THD)

The THDi generated by double conversion rectifiers, VFDs and UPS can exceed 40% in unmitigated installations. It injects heat into transformers and neutrals, reduces the useful life of insulation and can trigger protections without a real failure.

IEC 61000-4-15 / EN 50160

Flicker and voltage variations (Pst / Plt)

Heavy starting loads — arc furnaces, welders, high-power compressors — produce rapid voltage variations that are perceived as flickering in lighting and generate errors in precision measurement and control equipment.

IEEE 1159/IEC 61000-4-11

Voltage sags / dips

A voltage gap of 100 ms to 70% can restart a PLC, fire a VFD or stop a bottling line. The source can be external (CFE network) or internal (high-power engine starters) and is only detected with continuous measurement. See voltage problems

IEEE 519/NMX-J-098-ANCE

Power factor and reactive power

A power factor below 0.90 generates reactive demand charges on CFE’s industrial tariff. Wrong correction — unyielding detonation capacitors — can amplify existing amplifications rather than reduce them.

IEC 61000-2-2 / CIGRE WG C4

Voltage imbalance and interharmonics

Imbalance greater than 2% in three-phase systems generates negative sequence currents that heat motors and generators by up to an additional 20%. Interharmonics — frequencies not multiples of 60 Hz — are characteristic of cycloconverters and variable frequency welders.

IEEE 519/IEC 61000-3-12

High frequency harmonics in solar inverters

Fast switching photovoltaic inverters (IGBT) inject high order harmonics (up to 40°–50°) that can interfere with industrial communication systems, distance relays and energy meters of the Class 0.2S type.

Voltage problems

Voltage drop and voltage gaps: what you see in plan and what is behind

Most stoppages attributed to “equipment failure” begin with voltage. These are the four cases we see the most and where to start with each one.

Voltage drop

‘Enginees do not start or delay speeding’

The voltage reaches the load point below the nominal: long or undersized conductors, transformer at its limit, poorly adjusted taps or direct starts. The motor torque falls with the square of the voltage: at 90% voltage about 81% of the torque remains. NOM-001-SEDE-2012 recommends not exceeding 3% in branch circuits and 5% in total.

How to correct voltage drop →

Voltage sags

‘The variable frequency drives are triggered’

A gap is a short drop in voltage, from half a cycle to a minute, almost always due to a failure in the network or the start of a large load. It lasts less than a blink, but enough for a variator to be protected by low voltage on the CD bus, a contactor to open or a PLC to restart.

Diagnosis

‘Paros without apparent cause’

If no one recorded what happened to the voltage in the second of the strike, the cause remains in mind. An energy quality analyzer records each event with time, depth and duration; when crossing it with the stopper, it is seen whether the origin is in the network, in the plant or in the adjustment of the equipment.

"We've already changed the equipment and it's still failing"

Changing the drive or PLC does not solve a voltage problem. The solution depends on the depth and duration of the event and what loads need to be protected: from a parameter setting to a regulator, a DVR, a UPS or a BESS.

Harmonic, reactive and flicker correction

Harmonics

Line reactances at VFD input

The most cost-efficient solution for variable frequency drives with 6-pulse rectifiers. A reactance of 3-5% reduces the THDi of the VFD from ~40% to ~30% without additional infrastructure. Recommended as first line when the number of VFDs is limited and the total THD is below the critical threshold.

Ideal for: plants with 1-10 medium drives, tight budget, quick integration.

Harmonics

LC Tuned Passive Filters

LC resonant circuits tuned to the dominant harmonic orders (5th, 7th, 11th, 13th). They offer high attenuation at tuning frequency with lower cost than active filters. The design must be validated against the system impedance to avoid parallel resonance with the CFE network or with existing FP banks.

Ideal for: large loads with a stable harmonic profile, heavy industry, steel, cement.

Harmonics + Reactive

Active Power Filter (APF / Active Power Filter)

Injects dynamic compensation current that cancels harmonics measured in real time (up to the 50th harmonic). No resonance with the network. It can simultaneously compensate reactive power and phase imbalance. The most flexible solution for variable loads or mixes of heterogeneous equipment in the same system.

Ideal for: plants with VFDs + UPS + rectifiers, variable process, pharmaceutical, precision manufacturing.

Power Factor

Capacitor bank with detuning reactance

Reactive power correction using automatic banks (APFC) with contactors or thyristors. The detuning reactance (typically 7% or 14%) moves the resonant frequency of the bank away from the dominant harmonics of the system, avoiding the amplifying effect generated by PF banks without detuning in environments with high harmonic distortion.

Ideal for: plants with cos φ < 0.90 and THDi < 15%, general industry, commerce, infrastructure.

Dynamic Reactive

STATCOM — static reactive power compensation

Static compensator based on VSC converter that injects or absorbs reactive power in milliseconds. Unlike capacitor banks, the STATCOM can operate with very low bus voltage (just when compensation is most needed) and does not generate resonance. Reduces flicker in pulsating loads such as arc furnaces and large starts.

Ideal for: arc furnaces, large compressors, steel mills, plants with high flicker and voltage variations.

Comprehensive protection

UPS with active filtering and line conditioner

Online double conversion UPS generate clean sinusoidal voltage at the output completely independent of harmonics in the input network. For highly sensitive production equipment — CNC machines, process servers, critical PLCs — the voltage conditioner combined with UPS protects against sags, flicker and mains harmonics, within the autonomy of its battery bank.

Ideal for: critical process loads, control rooms, high precision measurement equipment.

Frequently asked questions

Doubts about electrical power quality

What is a drop in voltage and how much is acceptable?
The Voltage drop is the difference between the voltage at the origin of the circuit and the one that reaches the load, due to the impedance of drivers and transformers. It is sustained: it lasts while the load is connected. NOM-001-SEDE-2012 recommends not to exceed 3 % in derived circuits or 5 % In the total of the feeder plus the derivative. Above that the engines lose torque (at 90% voltage, about 81 % of the torque), start with difficulty and heat.
What is a voltage gap (voltage sag) and how is it different from a voltage drop?
A Voltage gap (V.A.S. o V.U.S.) is a brief reduction in voltage, typically between 10% and 90% of the nominal value, lasting from half a cycle to one minute (IEEE 1159). The voltage drop is permanent while the load operates; the gap is an event. The gaps usually come from failures in the CFE network or from starts of large motors within the plant, and are only detected with an analyzer that records continuously.
Why do frequency changers shoot without apparent failure?
The most common cause is a Voltage gap: the drive's DC bus goes low, the equipment is protected by low voltage and stops, although the network recovers in milliseconds. Other causes are transients due to capacitor maneuvering and harmonics that heat up the rectifier. A diagnosis with a power quality analyzer makes it possible to link each trip with its event and decide if a power adjustment is sufficient. • The European Commission has been invited to take part in either an UPS, DVR or BESS is needed.
What is an energy quality analyzer and what does it record?
It is a measuring device that is installed on the panel, without interrupting operation, and continuously records voltage and current. The Class A analysers (IEC 61000-4-30) capture voltage dips and sags, interruptions, surges, transients, harmonics, flicker and power factor, with the exact time and waveform of each event. These records are the basis of the voltage quality diagnosis.
What is the best for voltage gaps: UPS, DVR or BESS?
It depends on the depth and duration of the holes and how much cargo needs to be protected. For PLC, control and instrumentation, a UPS. For a complete process line with split-second gaps, a DVR injects missing voltage without charging large batteries. If there are also interruptions from seconds to minutes or a economical case on demand, a BESS When the hole is caused by an internal start, a soft starter or variator removes it at the source.
What do they measure in an energy quality diagnosis?
We measure Voltage drop and interruptions, Voltage gaps (voltage sags or dips), THDi and THDv (current and voltage harmonic distortion), Power Factor real and displacement, Fender (Pst and Plt according to IEC 61000-4-15), voltage imbalance, transients and frequency variations. The measurement is carried out at the critical points of the system for a minimum of 7 continuous days with class A analyzers.
What standard does it apply to Aronics in Mexico?
The international reference standard most used in industrial projects in Mexico is IEEE 519-2022, which sets limits of THDi and THDv depending on the short circuit level at the point of common coupling (PCC). They also apply IEC 61000-3-2 E IEC 61000-3-12 according to the power of the generating equipment, and NMX-J-098-ANCE For solar parks connected to the CFE network, the Network Code of the National Parks is additionally applied.
How long does the measurement campaign last?
The minimum recommended measurement is 7 continuous days to capture load variations between shifts and working days vs. weekend. For plants with highly variable processes (safra, pharmaceutical batch, seasonal production campaigns) we recommend 14 or 28 days The measurement to make the profile representative of the worst operational case.
Are frequency variable frequency drives the only generators of the Aronics?
No. The main harmonic generators in an industrial plant are: frequency changers (VFDs) with 6 and 12 pulse rectifiers, Double conversion UPS, Battery charging rectifiers, Arch furnaces, switched power supplies in automation equipment, photovoltaic inverters and variable frequency welding machines. Identification of the dominant source is the basis for correctly sizing the solution.
Does the correction of the Aronics improve the power factor?
It depends on the solution. The Active filters (APF) correct Aronics and can compensate for reactive power simultaneously, improving both THDi and the total power factor (PF, not only cos passive filters specific amonics but do not compensate for fundamental reactive. Unfiltered capacitor banks can make the problem of the Aronics worse if they are not designed with the correct de-intonation.
Does diagnosis require stopping production?
No. The installation of the power quality analyzers is carried out on the conductors in operation using Hall effect current clamps without direct contact with energized conductors. The diagnosis does not interrupt the operation of the plant. Installation of the correction solution (filters, banks or APF) may require a short shutdown window for connection to the main board.
How long does it take to see results after installing the filters?
The results are immediate after commissioning. A active filter (APF) is compensated in microseconds; the THDi reduction is measurable in the first network cycle. The final validation is done with a second 7-day post-installation measurement campaign to confirm compliance with the IEEE 519 limits and the elimination of CFE penalties.
