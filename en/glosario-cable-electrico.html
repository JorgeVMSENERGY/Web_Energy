Glossary of industrial electrical cable

Key terms for specifying, comparing and supplying industrial electrical cable: voltages, insulation, standards, constructions and field concepts. A quick reference for project engineers, procurement teams and technical areas.

A

  • Ampacity

    Allowable current

    Maximum allowable cable current under specific installation conditions (ambient temperature, installation method, bundling). The catalog value is theoretical — the actual value depends on the project correction factors.

  • Shielding

    Metal screen

    Conductive layer that surrounds the insulation to confine the electric field and provide a path for the fault current. In medium and high voltage it is executed with copper tape or braid; In EHV cables the screen is accompanied by a metal sheath that adds water tightness. A "shielded" cable without a continuous shield to ground does not fulfill its function.

    See also: Metal sheath.

  • Armor

    Galvanized steel wires

    Layer of galvanized steel wires that mechanically protects the cable against impacts and crushing. It is mandatory for directly buried installation and recommended in ducts with risk of mechanical damage. In chemically aggressive soils it is combined with HDPE cover.

    Catalog: Extra high voltage cables.

B

  • BHT

    Bottom Hole Temperature

    Well bottom temperature. It is the main criterion for selecting the ESP cable family (Arteria 180 °F, 205 °F , 290 °F or 400 °F and 450 °F). Use the real BHT, not surface temperature, to avoid premature insulation failures.

    Application: ESP cables for oil & gas.

C

  • Class 2

    Fixed stranded conductor

    Standard flexibility stranded conductor. It is the typical conductor of power cables in fixed installations (substations, feeders, distribution lines). It is not designed for continuous winding/unwinding.

  • Class 5

    Very flexible driver

    Highly flexible conductor made up of fine wires. It is used in moving cables, trailing cables, cables for moving equipment and photovoltaic cables (PV1-F). Allows smaller curvature radii and supports bending cycles.

    Application: PV1-F photovoltaic cables.

  • Cover

    Outer jacket

    Non-conductive outer layer that protects the cable from the environment: humidity, chemical agents, UV radiation and abrasion. The material is decided by the environment, not the price: HDPE for aggressive soil and direct burial, PVC for general use, LSZH where smoke and halogens are the risk.

    See also: LSZH.

E

  • EHV

    Extra High Voltage

    Cables with voltages greater than 150 kV (typically 150–500 kV), governed by IEC 62067. Require detailed engineering, type testing, pre-molded splices and manufacturing times of 16 to 28 weeks. The VMS Energy catalog covers the band from 66 to 400 kV, which covers both standards: IEC 60840 up to 150 kV and IEC 62067 above.

    Catalog: Extra high voltage cables 66-400 kV.

  • EMC

    Electromagnetic compatibility

    Control and instrumentation cables with shielding designed so that electrical noise from the environment - drives, starters, radio frequency - is not coupled to the signal, and so that the signal does not contaminate neighboring equipment. Common in control rooms and in pipelines that run alongside frequency converters.

    Catalog: control cables and special applications.

  • Splicing

    Union of two tranches

    Two cable sections are combined to rebuild conductor, insulation, screen and cover. In medium and high voltage it runs with pre-moulded voltage class kits and is tested before energize. Each splice is a potential failure point: the laying engineering seeks to reduce its number, not just run them well.

    Service: Industrial laying and wiring.

  • EPR

    Ethylene-Propylene Rubber

    Flexible elastomeric insulation with good performance in humid environments and mobile applications. It operates at 90 °C in normal regime and 250 °C in short circuit. It is the preferred option for trailing cables in mining and moving equipment.

    Comparison: XLPE vs EPR for power cable.

  • ESP

    Electric Submersible Pump

    Electric pump system used in oil wells to bring fluids to the surface. ESP cable feeds the pumpable engine and is one of the most exposed components to temperature, pressure and corrosive agents of the well.

    Catalog: ESP Arteria cables (180°F to 450°F).

F

  • Correction factor

    KT · KA · Kρ

    Multiplier that lowers the catalog ampacity to the actual conditions of the project: ambient temperature (kT), circuit clustering (kA) and ground thermal resistivity (kρ). The admissible current in the field is Itables × kT × kA × kρ. Six three-phase cables together in a tray can lower it by 33%.

    Calculation: selection of industrial electric cable.

H

  • H.V.

    High Voltage

    High voltage cables, above 36 kV and up to 150 kV. Applicable in interconnection of substations, critical industrial lines and transmission projects. Reference standard: IEC 60840, covering extruded cables from 36 to 150 kV; Above 150 kV, IEC 62067 mandates.

    Catalog: Extra high voltage cables.

K

  • Kapton

    Polyimide

    Polyimide film used as insulation and covering in ESP cables of the Arteria 400 and 450 families. It offers resistance superior to H2S and corrosive fluids, where polypropylene of families 180 and 205 is not suitable.

    Catalog: ESP cables for well.

L

  • LAPEM

    Equipment and Materials Testing Laboratory

    Laboratory of the Federal Electricity Commission (CFE). Issues technical reference specifications for cables and equipment in projects with interconnection to the CFE network. Any project that connects to CFE must review the current LAPEM specifications.

  • LSZH

    Low Smoke Zero Halogen

    Flame retardant cover that emits little smoke in the event of fire and does not release toxic halogen gases. Mandatory in confined spaces, tunnels, hospitals, public buildings and marine platforms. Helukabel and other European manufacturers offer a wide range of LSZH.

M

  • MTO

    Take-Off Material

    Quantified list of materials generated from the detailed engineering of a project. On EPC projects, the cable MTO is synchronized with the construction schedule to coordinate milestone deliveries, not with the supplier's inventory.

  • MV

    Medium Voltage

    Medium voltage cables, between 1 kV and 36 kV. It is the most common range in industrial distribution: substations, feeders, load centers and plants. Reference standard: IEC 60502.

    Catalog: MV/HV power cable.

N

  • NMX-J

    Mexican Standard — J series (electrotechnics)

    Mexican standard applicable to electrical cables and conductors. National manufacturers such as Condumex and Viakon produce under NMX-J. It is the reference for compliance with NOM-001-SEDE in low and medium voltage installations with a national component.

  • NOM-001-HEADQUARTERS

    Electrical installations (use)

    Official Mexican Standard for electrical installations, mandatory in Mexico. Governs selection of conductors, conduits, protections, supports and grounding. All supply and laying is specified under NOM-001-SEDE, along with the IEC or NMX-J standard that corresponds to the cable.

    See also: NMX-J.

P

  • PV1-F

    Direct current solar cable

    Flexible tinned copper DC solar cable—Class 5 conductor—with halogen-free cross-linked polyolefin insulation and cover. Standards: BS EN 50618, 2Pfg 1169/08.2007 and IEC 60228 Class 5. The standard PV1-F is not suitable for direct buried installation: underground sections require additional cover or protective pipe.

    Catalog: PV1-F photovoltaic cable.

R

  • Radius of curvature

    Minimum bending radius

    Minimum curvature that a cable admits without damaging insulation or screen, expressed as a multiple of its external diameter. In MV power cable, 12 to 15 times the diameter is handled during laying (IEC 60502-2); on PV1-F photovoltaic cable, 4 times. It is one of the three construction conditions, along with the minimum installation temperature and the maximum pulling tension.

    Service: Industrial laying and wiring.

T

  • Termination

    Cable tip

    Prepared end of the cable to connect to equipment or a bar. It reconstructs the control of the electric field that the screen was providing along the cable, and that is why it is executed with kits of a defined voltage class —15, 25 or 35 kV. It is documented with a test protocol per circuit before commissioning.

    See also: Splicing.

  • Trailing cable

    Trailing cable

    Cable designed to support continuous winding and unwinding, typically on reels. It is used in mobile mining equipment (shovels, drills), port cranes and heavy industrial machinery. Requires Class 5 conductor and EPR insulation.

U

  • U0/U

    Nominal cable voltage

    Designation of the nominal cable voltage expressed in kV. U0 is the effective phase-earth voltage and U The effective voltage phase-phase. Examples: 6/10 kV, 12/20 kV, 18/30 kV, 64/110 kV. The selection should also include the maximum voltage of the system (Um).

V

  • Metal sheath

    EHV Cable Layer 7

    Exterior metal layer of the extra high voltage cables: provides watertightness and closes the confinement of the field. The construction chosen—plain or corrugated aluminum, corrugated copper, lead, or corrugated stainless steel—determines the weight, bend radius, and cost of the cable.

    Exploded view: Extra high voltage cables.

X

  • LCA

    Cross-Linked Polyethylene

    Thermostable insulation with high dielectric strength. It operates at 90 °C in normal regime and 250 °C in short circuit. It is the preferred insulation for power cables in fixed installations: substations, feeders and transmission lines. Its counterpart in flexibility is the EPR.

    Comparison: XLPE vs EPR for power cable.
